# object-sync — absolute world position + rotation for a droppable prop (Module)

A prop that can be set down anywhere in the world and is in the same place for everyone, including a player who joins after it was dropped: a two-stage contact measure quantizes its world transform on the wearer's client and `word-channel` replicates the bits, with no physbone, no Rigidbody, and no synced position of its own. The packaged novelty is the redundant fine stage — a second, bias-cancelling readout wide enough to absorb the coarse stage's error — which puts millimetre placement anywhere in a ±8192 m world inside **29 synced bits** — a 28-bit transport wire plus the one enable bit — refreshed end to end in ~0.7 s.

Three built configurations ship, each with its own regenerated document, compiled controller and prefab, so a download is usable without this workspace: the entry root (one object, full rotation, 72 rotation bits on the wire), `y/` (one object, heading only, 24 rotation bits, the same 29 synced bits total) and `y_double/` (two objects, heading only, time-multiplexed onto one measure rig, 30 synced bits: a 29-bit wire plus the shared enable). One build per avatar, whichever variant — all three share the parameter prefix and the collision tags, so the single-instance rule under **Seam** spans them.

## Provenance

VRLabs **Custom-Object-Sync** (MIT, © VRLabs) is the studied ancestor. Two of its idioms are re-derived here: the successive-approximation threshold walk that turns an analog reading into bits, and the setter tree that drives a constraint's `PositionOffset` from decoded values. Its receive side is rejected wholesale — a multi-frame decode walk is exactly what a culling pause interrupts, so every decode here is a blend tree holding no state between frames — and so is its physics world-pin and its six rotation physbones. The transport is `vrc-patterns/word-channel`, whose protocol descends from VRCFury's Parameter Compressor. Nothing is ported from either; both are credited as ancestors.

## Interface

**The consumer surface is two roles, and each is one node**: `Sync_Target` is yours to drive, `Sync` is the entry's to drive and yours to use — Custom-Object-Sync's `SyncPosition Target` / `SyncPosition` pair under the same names. Mount your prop under `Sync`, or list `Sync` as a source in a multiplexer of your own; for a carried prop add your constraint sources to `Sync_Target`'s shipped (empty) constraint. Nothing else is an install step.

The split is a two-writer rule, and it is what every composed behavior hangs off: the entry never binds `Sync_Target` (`--check` holds that), and you never bind anything under `Sync` — **binding**, not reading, so listing `Sync` as a source in a constraint of your own costs the rule nothing (§Composing against Sync). Carry, stow, world-drop, or a RemyDoll-grade authority multiplexer are all constraints and clips you compose against `Sync_Target`; the entry measures it and reconstructs, continuously, with no modes. **`Sync` is correct on both clients, which is the point of the split** — the wearer's copy rides `Sync_Target` with no decode in the path, a remote's rides the reconstruction once it is valid, and the module owns that choice so no consumer has to make it. While the prop is carried with Enable on, remotes track the reconstruction, ~1.3 s behind the true hand; drop is where this entry converges exactly, carry is where a composed remote-side swap could someday do better. No per-object sync bit, because there is no runtime object index — multi-object is a list in `generate.py`'s CONFIG, resolved at generation time into that many objects' worth of words.

- **Drop, as shipped**: the `Drop` toggle (a VRCFury `Toggle` on `Sync_Target`, its own synced bool — one bit on top of the entry's 29, deleted with the component if you never drop) animates `Sync_Target`'s constraint `FreezeToWorld`. On a sourced constraint every 0→1 edge re-captures — pin where it stands, release back to the source (`../../docs/runtime.md` §Constraints owns the measurement) — so with a hand source wired, Drop-on freezes the prop in world for the wearer instantly and the wire walks every other client to the same millimetre. **Wire a source first**: source-less, `FreezeToWorld` captures once, permanently, and nothing animatable resets it. Keep Enable on while dropping — the freeze is per-client, so only the reconstruction, not the frozen input, is the same place for everyone.

- **Params in**: `ObjectSync/Enable` (bool, synced, **unsaved**, default off) — the master gate, on `globalParams`, so an outside driver or OSC can reach it under that bare name. Nothing else: the module measures a transform, not a parameter. `Rig/Prop/Coarse/Sender`, `Rig/Prop/Fine/Sender` and `Rig/Prop/Rot/Holder` name `Sync_Target` directly — **all three constraints, carrying four contact senders** (the rotation holder has two: `MarkA`, `MarkB`), **and no assertion can hold that for you** (they live in the hand-maintained prefab, out of `--check`'s reach), so a sender left on some other transform is the one silent way to make the stages measure different objects. To measure something deeper than the input — a grab bone inside your prop — source `Sync_Target` from it rather than re-pointing a sender.
- **Menu**: one bare VRCFury `Toggle` (`CONVENTIONS.md` §The Interface stanza) — `Object Sync` on `ObjectSync/Enable` from the controller's menu block — plus the composed `Drop` toggle on `Sync_Target` (VRCFury-prefixed param, deletable composition rather than module surface). No menu asset. The module is single-instance per avatar regardless, for the collision-tag reason under **Seam**.
- **Params out** (every client): `ObjectSync/Ch/Acquired` (bool, **not synced**, on `globalParams`) — the transport's own correctness output, *this client's receiver has applied a complete word table since the last animator load or distance-cull pause*. The entry declares no validity param beside it. It is what a consumer hides or blends their own stand-in on; the module shows nothing of its own and hides nothing of yours. It reads **false on the wearer** (which runs no receiver) and stays **true on a remote with `Enable` off** (which never gated the wire), so the predicate a consumer actually wants is `IsLocal OR (ObjectSync/Enable AND ObjectSync/Ch/Acquired)` — drop either added term and a remote parked on `Sync_Target` reads as trustworthy. Never gate on `ObjectSync/Ch/Cycle`, word-channel's liveness counter: it answers a different question, its thresholds are apply-discipline-dependent, and being off `globalParams` it is instance-prefixed anyway (`../word-channel` §Interface owns both propositions and what each one is *not*). Also out: `Prop/PX/{C,F}` + `Prop/PX/{C0..C4,F0..F3}` and the same per axis, `Prop/R{A,B}/{X,Y,Z}` + four bools each — the word table, driven by the receiver on remotes and by the encode layers on the wearer. Everything else is `scratch:`.
- **Wire** (word-channel's synced params, all unsaved): 3 index bools + 2 8-bit int slots + 9 bool slots = **28 bits**, 6 batches, ~0.70 s full refresh at 60 fps. Plus `ObjectSync/Enable`, the entry's own synced bit, for **29 synced bits total** — the wire is what the transport costs, the total is what the avatar pays. The counter spans one loop, not two: `indexLoops` divides a pause-alias probability that `atomic: batch` does not have, so a second loop would cost an index bit and 18 `Sync` states for nothing.
- **Seam**: VRCFury `FullController` on the prefab root, `basis: mount-root` ↔ the FullController default `rootBindingsApplyToAvatar: 0` — every clip binding paths through the module's own rig, so the internal hierarchy names in **Rig** below are load-bearing. `globalParams` is exactly `ObjectSync/Enable` and `ObjectSync/Ch/Acquired` — the first because the module's own `Toggle` drives it by name, the second because a consumer must be able to bind it by name and a param VRCFury has prefixed is unreachable to them; every other param takes an instance prefix, `ObjectSync/Ch/Cycle` included. That makes the *parameters* instance-safe and nothing else — the four collision tags are fixed strings VRCFury's prefixing does not reach, and two copies park their contact clusters at the same point, so a second instance needs a regeneration with its own tags, not a second drop of the prefab. **Extend this list by scoping, not by enumerating**: a consumer needing more of the module's namespace writes `ObjectSync/*` plus `!` exclusions rather than adding names one at a time, because an enumeration silently goes stale when this entry renames a param — the stale entry is dropped without a word while the new name takes an instance prefix (`../../docs/gimmicks.md` §Packaging owns the grammar). The two names above are the published interface and stay enumerated *here*; scoping is for the consumer's own list.

**Retired:** `ObjectSync/Sync_Valid`, replaced by `ObjectSync/Ch/Acquired`. It is not a rename — the proposition inverted. `Sync_Valid` read true on the wearer where `Acquired` reads false forever, so a consumer porting across needs `IsLocal OR (ObjectSync/Enable AND ObjectSync/Ch/Acquired)`, all three terms, not a substituted name.
- **Dependencies**: **compose `anti-cull` alongside** — the decode runs only while a remote client evaluates the wearer's animator, and a view-culled wearer's prop freezes where it was last decoded. Distance-hide is not defeatable and is a declared limit, not a defect.
- **Required assets**: `assets/World.prefab` — the never-instantiated prefab `Rig` sources, which is what makes the frame the same world origin on every client. A bare origin node; do not instantiate or delete it.

## Composing against Sync

The shape both known consumers converged on independently — the operator's own doll rig and this entry's demo avatar — and the one to reach for whenever the prop has more than one authority:

    World.prefab pin (your rig root, VRCParentConstraint, Locked)
      └─ mux node          VRCPosition + VRCRotation, N sources, weights animated
         └─ damper node    VRCPosition + VRCRotation, sources (mux, self), per channel
            └─ content

`Sync` is one source in that mux, beside your hand anchors and whatever else places the prop — not the prop's parent. Nine sources is a real number, not a hypothetical one. Weight it in on the three-term predicate under **Params out** and out again when the pose is not yours to trust. That predicate is deliberately **stricter than `Follow`'s own latch**, which holds the reconstruction through a cull resume until the table re-certifies — mirror `Follow` instead only if you want your stand-in to hold through that window too. Pin your rig root at **zero** source offset, like `Rig`'s own pin: the shipping client scales a source's `ParentPositionOffset` by the avatar's scale factor (the park row under **Rig** owns the mechanism), so any world offset you need belongs on a child transform under the pin, never on the pin itself — and keep that child under the scale pin, which is what holds its local position at true metres.

**Damping belongs on your damper node, never inside this entry.** A module-side damper could only smooth its own source, leaving every other mode in your mux unsmoothed, and two dampers in series on the sync path is worse than either alone. It would also cost a channel: position wants lag where rotation usually wants none, and that is two weights, not one. Custom-Object-Sync damps because its reconstruction jitters continuously; this one lands `atomic: batch` at ~1.5 mm and steps once per wire cycle, so smoothing it is cosmetic and buys the smoothing with τ × speed of further lag on the path already worst-off for latency. `../spring-damping` owns the law and the time constant.

**A damper's parent must be inertially static; its target may move freely.** That is why the tier above it is pinned to `World.prefab` — the same pin `Rig` carries here, and for the same reason. A damper whose parent moves is dragged by its parent's motion, measured at ~21× the per-frame parent step at a 1:0.05 weight.

## How it works

**Position, per axis, in two stages.** A `VRCPositionConstraint` squeezes the prop toward the rig anchor — anchor weight `1 − g`, prop weight `g = coarseHalfSpan/range` — so the whole ±8192 m range lands inside ±2.75 m of a 6 m face receiver. An `IsLocal` successive-approximation walk (one state pair per bit, accepted above, rejected below) turns that reading into 13 bits naming a 2 m **cell**. The walk's accumulated cell index places a second anchor at that cell's centre, and a sender constrained to the prop is read at 1:1 by face receivers riding that anchor, which a second walk turns into 12 more bits. Anchor, receiver and sender share one hierarchy, so the float32 displacement the avatar's own distance from the world origin introduces appears identically on both sides of the subtraction and cancels bit-exactly — measured identical to the last bit with the avatar parked at 0 m and at 5 km.

**Why 13+12 and not 13+11.** The coarse stage does *not* cancel that displacement: at 8 km its world error runs to 1.16 m, more than half a cell, so the cell it picks can be one boundary out. The fine field is therefore **redundant** — it spans the cell plus twice the coarse error (4.4 m), not the cell (2 m) — so an off-by-one cell is still reconstructed exactly, at the cost of one bit per axis and no correction pass anywhere. That 4.4 m field sits inside the 6 m face with 0.8 m spare at each end, which is what keeps the readout off the two ways a face receiver lies: it saturates at 1.0 for about 20 mm past the face plane, and past that it reads exactly 0 — the same value as "nothing overlaps".

**Rotation without trig or physbones.** A rotation-only holder carries two markers on orthogonal 1 m arms; three face receivers per marker give six components, sent raw. Every client rebuilds the orientation with a `VRCAimConstraint` pair: `UpAim` aims +Y at proxy B, and `Recon` — **its child**, so the solver is forced to resolve `UpAim` first in the same frame — aims +Z at proxy A with `WorldUpType` **ObjectRotationUp** against `UpAim`. `ObjectUp` looks like the right token and silently degenerates to world up; it is never correct here. The `y` preset is a strict subset of the same rig — marker A's X and Z components on the wire, one `Recon` aim constraint with `WorldUpType` **Vector** `(0,1,0)`, no second marker and no `UpAim` — and it sends components rather than the heading itself because a parameter driver reads 0 from an AAP, so no walk can quantize a number a blend tree computed. Its two components are not two values, though: they are one heading. The two walks still run in **parallel** — serializing them would add a whole 12-bit ladder to every measure cycle of the configuration this preset exists for — but neither writes a word. Each ends by raising a staged-done flag, and a two-state **commit barrier** publishes all 24 bits in one driver frame once both flags are up, then lowers them, releasing the walks. It costs two frames, two scratch bools and one layer, and nothing in cycle time: the rotation path runs 17 frames against the position walk's 34, so the slice is unchanged. Full mode's six components genuinely are independent readings from two markers, so a torn set of them is a stale orientation rather than one no marker held, and they keep a walk and a commit each.

**The wire, and why nothing tears.** Each axis's coarse byte, fine byte and nine bools share one `group:`, so word-channel pins them into one batch and `atomic: batch` applies them together: an adjacent-cell coarse always lands with its matched fine, which reconstructs the same position, so cell-boundary flicker needs no hysteresis anywhere. Each rotation component's byte and four bools share a cross-kind group the same way, and `y` mode's two heading components share one group as well. The walks are multi-frame and that is safe — they run only on the wearer's own client, which cannot cull itself — but the handoff to the wire must not be, because word-channel's sender copies the word params on its own clock and would happily latch a half-written axis. So the walks fill scratch staging and one `Commit` state driver-copies a whole axis, all eleven words, in a single frame. The rotation walks are the same rule applied to whatever counts as one value: a commit each in full mode, and in `y` two parallel walks handing both heading limbs to one commit barrier.

**Two anchors, not one.** The measurement anchor rides the walk's running cell index — a staged value, mid-cycle, local. The display anchor rides the committed word and is side-agnostic: the wearer decodes its own prop from the same words a remote does. They cannot be one node, because sharing would mean committing the coarse word before the fine walk has run, which is exactly the torn axis the commit exists to prevent.

**Who drives the prop.** `Sync`'s constraint is the entry's whole reach into the consumer's hierarchy, and the `Follow` layer moves weight between its two sources. **It is a latch, not a predicate held both ways**: an AnyState rung with `canTransitionToSelf: false` engages the reconstruction on `!IsLocal AND ObjectSync/Enable AND ObjectSync/Ch/Acquired`, and the only exit tests `Enable` alone — so once engaged it holds through whatever `Acquired` does after, and a receiver falling to `Lost` mid-session does not drop the prop back to its target. Add a rung here without deciding what it does to that, and you un-latch it. Enable-off, a pre-gate remote and a culled decode still collapse to one case on the way in — `Sync` rides `Sync_Target` — so the module hides nothing of yours and shows nothing of its own. A layer rather than a rung of the enable's blend tree, because `IsLocal` is a bool built-in and a tree reads 0 from it forever. The `Acquired` term is the late-joiner gate: a fresh clone's word table is all zeros, zero is cell 0, and cell 0 is the corner of the range, so engaging before a whole table has arrived would show the corner — and taking the transport's own certification means no threshold here shifts if the apply discipline changes. Three states over two clips, because the wearer and a pre-gate remote hold the same pose and opposite validity, and one state cannot say both; none of the three drives a parameter, since the proposition a consumer needs is already `Acquired`. The wearer's copy never rides the decode: it moves under whatever the consumer's own system gives it, with no decode in the path and no constraint of ours between the carry constraint and the prop.

**The enable, and what it does not gate.** `ObjectSync/Enable` is one synced bit, so wearer and remotes park together. Off, a 1D tree on it deactivates the three measure subtrees; each measuring layer takes an AnyState rung into a `Parked` state whose driver clears that layer's staging, residual, cell index and sense params — in that order, because a deactivated sensing component does not fall to zero, it *freezes* at its last live reading, and only a clear written after the deactivation sticks. What Enable does **not** gate is the wire: word-channel's 28 bits are allocated whether or not the prop is out, so gating it would save nothing and would cost the re-enable a full re-lock. The word table is left holding the last committed pose for the same reason — re-enabling shows the prop where it was, not at the origin. One consequence, intended: because the receiver keeps decoding through the toggle, `ObjectSync/Ch/Acquired` stays true across an Enable-off, and flipping Enable back on **engages on that frame** rather than waiting out a cycle.

**Coming back up is where a clear turns into a lie.** A cleared sense param reads 0, and 0 quantizes to cell 0 — the far corner of the range — so a walk that samples before its reactivated receivers have re-acquired publishes a confident wrong answer rather than nothing. Walks therefore gate on **receiver liveness**, not on a timer: a 0 reading is never legal in-volume, which is the edge guard's second job, and every walk-entry transition additionally requires the very params that walk is about to sample to read above a threshold derived from that same guard. Re-acquisition measured 7 frames in the emulator, is unbounded in principle, and the gate does not care. The road to any `Commit` likewise runs through every bit of the walk. Both are transition structure rather than a timing argument, and `--check` asserts both. The multi-object `Slice` layer is the same discipline at slice granularity — deactivate every other object's rig, clear, wait for this one's receivers to come live, then unblock its walks — with a bounded skip so a rig that never wakes yields its slice instead of starving the others, and a second AnyState rung making a walk that loses the rig mid-flight abandon rather than commit limbs measured against somebody else's senders.

**A gate is only safe where waiting can end.** A liveness wait survives exactly when its condition can be restored without the machine advancing past the wait, and one wait fails that test: the fine receiver rides the measurement anchor, and only a fresh coarse commit moves it. Teleport the prop out of its current cell while the walk is past the coarse stage and the fine sender leaves its box for good, so that wait blocks on a stage it can no longer reach — measured in a venue as 2 of 5 instantaneous teleports stalling the position stage indefinitely while rotation, whose markers cannot leave their boxes, kept tracking. The fine wait therefore carries the machine's one escape: after `fineEscapeFrames` of dead reading it abandons to `Idle`, which restages the cell, snaps the anchor and restores overlap. Every other wait is on a source that restores itself — the coarse receiver's box spans the whole working volume, and the rotation markers ride rigid arms — and `generate.py`'s liveness audit records which is which, with `--check` holding the document to it in both directions, so a new gate cannot be added without an entry saying why waiting there ends.

The failure mode that buys is the one this entry is built around: a rig that never wakes leaves its walk waiting and the wire holds the last committed pose. Nothing publishes a position it did not measure, and nothing waits on a reading that will never come.

**Empirical constants** (measured in the emulator; re-measure before changing any of them):

| Constant | Value | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Coarse readout error, contact space | 0.39 mm worst case, avatar 4–8 km from world origin | `coarseNoise` in CONFIG; amplified by `range/coarseHalfSpan` to 1.16 m of world error, and the fine field is widened by twice that |
| Fine readout | float32-exact, zero noise; ≤1 frame stale under motion | why the fine stage sets system precision and the coarse stage's error costs bits, not accuracy |
| Fine-anchor settle | 1 frame to the cell centre, 2–4 frames to a coherent reading | `settleFrames` = 4 |
| Rotation error at 12 bits/component | 0.032° mean, 0.12° max, at 90° marker separation | `rotBits`; the bar it beats is Custom-Object-Sync's 0.176° |
| End-to-end position error, 2 m to 9.6 km | 0.7–1.8 mm, per axis inside `[−1.3, 0] mm` | the precision floor: the walk floors rather than rounds, so the residual is one-sided. Two terms make it up — one fine LSB (1.07 mm) of quantization, plus the float32 ulp at range (~0.98 mm at 8192 m) — and together they can exceed a single LSB, which is why the measured band runs past 1.07 mm. More bits would move neither term |

**Costs.** 377 states and 15 layers for the root build (261/12 and 516/19 for `y/` and `y_double/` — read them off the compile line, which prints both, rather than off the previous figure plus a delta); 34 frames (~0.57 s) per local measure cycle, plus however long the receivers take to come live after an unpark, ~0.70 s per wire refresh, ~1.3 s worst case end to end. A two-object build costs a slice each instead, ~0.8 s per slice. Twelve local-only receivers per object, zero physbones. The design also has a speed ceiling: sustained authority motion faster than roughly the fine field over one measure-plus-wire interval (≈ 7–8 m/s at 60 fps for the committed geometry) walks the fine sender out of its box every cycle, so the escape restages continuously and the display holds the last good commit rather than tracking — measured at 12 m/s as a clean hold, 1.5 m/s as normal ~1.3 s-lag tracking. A droppable prop never reaches it; a projectile consumer is out of scope by construction, not by tuning. `generate.py --check` holds regeneration byte-identical for all three committed documents, pins each against the one its `built/` was compiled from, and asserts packing, commit reachability, the liveness gate on every walk entry, the slice sequencing and the reconstruction shapes across all three — including that no driver anywhere reads or writes an AAP param, which is the shape of the defect that cost y-mode its first design.

## Verifying the install

Cheapest observable: **move `Sync_Target` with the wearer standing away from the world origin and facing away from +Z**. On the wearer, `Sync` must track it frame-for-frame; any lag there means the local branch is riding something it must not. On a remote clone, `Sync` reaches the same world pose inside about a second — millimetres of position, hundredths of a degree of rotation, the same value on both sides because both decode the same words — and `ObjectSync/Ch/Acquired` must be true on that clone. Its two other readings look like faults and are not: **false on the wearer**, and **true on a clone with Enable off** — Enable changes which source `Sync` rides, never whether the table is real. The off-origin, off-axis stance is the part that is easy to skip and the part that catches the whole class: a rig frame that has quietly stayed avatar-relative is indistinguishable from a correct one at the origin, so read `Rig` while you are there and require exactly `(0, 0, 0)` with identity rotation on both clients, with `Rig/Prop` at exactly `(73, 932, 233)` and the pin's source offset zero — and lossy scale exactly `(1, 1, 1)` with the avatar scaled away from upload height (av3emu: `EnableAvatarScaling` + `AvatarHeight` on the local runtime), which is the check that catches a lost scale pin: unpinned, the fine receivers read exactly 0 at any forced scale while coarse and rotation stay green, the same verdict a world eye-height clamp produces in game. Local and clone disagreeing by more than quantization is the decode reading a different word table, not the wire being slow; a clone's `Sync` frozen while `VF##_ObjectSync/Ch/Cycle` climbs is the rig rather than the wire (`../word-channel`'s own §Verifying owns that reading, and why the counter carries a per-build instance prefix). On a cold join `ObjectSync/Ch/Acquired` is false and the prop rides `Sync_Target` for **one to two loops (~0.6–1.3 s)** before the reconstruction takes over already at its synced pose — a late joiner never sees the range corner at all. **The spread is the normal reading, not a fault**: under `atomic: batch` the receiver re-locks at any counter value, and only a join landing on a batch head certifies at that loop's tail. For the drop path: with a hand source on `Sync_Target` and Enable on, Drop-on must freeze the wearer's prop on that frame and walk a clone to the same world pose inside the usual ~1.3 s; Drop-off must snap the wearer's prop back to the hand on that frame — any wearer-side lag on either edge means something other than `FreezeToWorld` is in the local path.

What the emulator structurally cannot show for this entry: everything `../word-channel` declares — its wire is this entry's wire — plus two that belong to the measure rig. **Contacts are never simulated against a remote clone**, so the twelve receivers are only ever exercised on the wearer's own client, and nothing here can reach a fault that needs another player's dynamics to appear — which is also why the park is an argument rather than a measurement: `(73, 932, 233)` is empty in a test scene by construction, where a real world may put geometry or another player's props through it. And **contact re-registration latency is the emulator's, not the client's**: a reactivated receiver takes 7 frames here, the shipping client is unmeasured, and the design gates on the reading rather than on that number precisely so the difference cannot matter. A third gap is the one the zero-offset pin closes: **the emulator SDK never scales a constraint source's offset by avatar scale, so a park that crept back onto any source offset would pass every emulator check and still misplace the prop cross-client.** The emulator-expressible shadow is the injected-scaling fixture: in play mode, apply the client's rule by hand — multiply every source offset on the remote clone's `Rig` constraints by a forced-scale factor (×1.35, say) — and require local/clone `Sync` agreement unchanged, which holds exactly when every offset is zero; injecting a park-sized offset first and watching agreement break by that vector is the teeth check. `generate.py --check` holds the static half — no animated source-space offset in any document, no nonzero source offset in any committed prefab — and only an in-client two-scale read (`../../docs/runtime.md` §Constraints) sees the real client behavior.
