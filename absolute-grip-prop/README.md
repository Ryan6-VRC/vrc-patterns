# absolute-grip-prop — a prop that lands in the same authored grip, in every hand, on every client (Module)

Grab the prop off the wearer and it snaps into your hand in the same grip every time, whichever hand takes it, and every client derives that grip from its own readout of the grabber's hand. Drop it and it stays where it was left, re-grabbable in place. Anyone can do this, the wearer included, and nobody installs anything. Position is the natively synced physbone grab with `snapToHand` on, so the tip is the client's own hand grab point. Orientation is re-derived on every client from the grabber's built-in `Hand` palm sender: eight box receivers on the tip solve the palm capsule's axis in blend trees, a two-receiver `HandL`/`HandR` gate decides the hand, a two-receiver `FingerIndex` cue decides which way the axis points, and the prop rides an **authored** grip pose under that frame. Nothing is captured at grab time, so repeated pickups have nothing to accumulate. Module total: **1 synced bit** (`AbsoluteGrip/Enable`), 12 contact receivers, no FinalIK.

**Provenance:** the remote-grabbable prop, its cage-on-the-tip acquisition, the hand gate on the tip, the snap-on grab and the per-hand grip pose are ThatFatKidsMom's Avatar-Prop (MIT), itself a derivative of VRLabs' Contact-Tracker. Avatar-Prop takes orientation from three finger trackers and a FinalIK aim solver and assumes a closed grip; this entry replaces those with one palm-capsule readout and two VRC aim constraints, pays one finger cue for the axis sign, and assumes no pose. The grab cell is `grab-prop`'s, the aim pair `object-sync`'s, the receiver conventions `box-tracker`'s. Deliberately not ported: Avatar-Prop's distance-too-far reset (`grab-prop`'s unlimited carry plus Enable-off recall covers it) and its left-hand grip mirror (§Interface says why a mirror is wrong).

## Interface

- **Params:** `AbsoluteGrip/Enable` (bool, in) — synced, **unsaved**; the menu front (VRCFury Toggle on the prefab root). Off is the reset: toggling off and on recalls a dropped prop home. Everything under `Palm/` is internal and takes the instance prefix; `globalParams` is the derived wildcard for the one published prefix.
- **Seam:** one VRCFury `FullController` on the prefab root playing two controllers, the glue first, then the readout; `basis: mount-root`, so the internal hierarchy names are load-bearing. `HomeAnchor` is an MA `BoneProxy` on Hips, referenced only as a constraint source. Both controllers and both params assets regenerate from `generate.py` and `CompileController`; the Toggle is hand-maintained.
- **Dependencies:** VRC SDK, VRCFury and Modular Avatar to build. **Compose `anti-cull` alongside** (its README §When a module needs this): the drop is replayed choreography and the orientation is contact tracking, both of which stop on a culled remote.
- **Required assets:** `assets/World.prefab`, the never-instantiated scale reference the cage pins to; do not instantiate or delete it. `Payload` is a placeholder sphere: swap it, keep it under `Container/Damped`, and author its local position against that node's origin, which lands on the latched hand's grip node in carry. That position is prop-frame and says where on the prop the hand closes. Where the origin sits relative to the grab point is hand-frame and belongs on the grip node, never on the payload: a prop-frame offset lands on a different side of the hand once the two grips differ.
- **The grip is yours to author.** `Frame/GripR` and `Frame/GripL` ship at identity: for a right hand the prop's +Z lies along the palm axis toward the thumb, its +Y points from the palm midpoint toward the tip, and its origin is on the grab point. Set each node's local rotation to how the prop should sit in that hand and its local position to trim the origin off the grab point (−Y is back toward the palm). Author the two independently, never one derived from the other: the two hands' sensed frames are reflections of each other, not rotations, so a grip that fixes which feature faces the thumb and which faces the palm reverses its third axis between hands, as a real object does. A feature that must instead point the same way in both hands, a barrel forward, costs the palm-facing feature its consistency, and the half turn about the handle that buys it goes on one node only. `generate.py --check` pins both rotations and both positions to the generator's constants, so author them there and in the prefab together.

## What it demonstrates

Recovering a **rotation** from another player's body with contacts, and making the grip that rides it **absolute**: the carry pose is `Frame · Grip`, a function of the grabber's current pose and an authored constant, with no dependence on what the prop was doing before the grab. The alternative, capturing the prop's pose relative to the sensed frame at each grab, has every client capture on its own readout at its own instant, so each pickup adds a per-client disagreement that nothing reconciles. Here a drop-time disagreement is replaced at the next grab, never compounded. Four things make that possible and none is visible from the artifacts.

**The readout is the palm capsule's axis, not a fingertip.** Every avatar carries a `Hand` sender: a capsule lying across the palm, rigid with the wrist, that no finger gesture moves. Eight face-proximity boxes in four opposed tetrahedral pairs read the capsule's extent along each direction, and the extents solve its half-length, its midpoint and the unsigned projections of its axis. The signs are the hard part: a line has a four-way sign ambiguity no static readout can break, so the readout holds one **oriented** sign pattern as animator state and hops only to a Hamming-neighbour pattern whose residual beats the held one by a margin. That is what makes the frame stable rather than merely correct; a prop that jumped tens of degrees whenever a projection crossed zero would be useless in a hand. It is all blend-tree arithmetic with no sqrt and no divide: the tetrahedral directions sum to zero, so a pattern's consistency residual is a plain signed sum, and the square root in the half-length solve is a 1D lookup. The frame is a `VRCAimConstraint` pair, an up helper aiming at the tip and a child aiming along the axis with `ObjectRotationUp` against it (`object-sync` §Rig owns why `ObjectUp` silently fails here).

**The tip is the client's hand grab point, and that makes the roll lever structural.** With `snapToHand` on, the grab pins the bone endpoint where the client puts a held object: seen in-game about a hand's thickness out of the palm along its normal, an observation, not a measurement, and every lever figure the entry was sized against is a constructed ball point on one base (the in-game checklist's first item reads the real one). So the tip sits a hand-sized lever off the palm axis on every grab and every client, and the frame's up direction, palm midpoint toward the tip, is well conditioned. There is no world-up fallback for an on-axis tip; what stands in its place is a refusal (§Limits), because there is no partially-correct absolute grip. The readout publishes the squared tip-to-midpoint distance, which is the lever only for a grab point near the palm's mid-plane: a point displaced along the axis reads a large distance with no lever, so the floor guards the observed grab point, not every on-axis one (the exact perpendicular test is a quartic in the readout's terms, two hops deeper, and was dropped for that).

**Handedness is a tag, never geometry.** The sensed features are a point and a directed line, which have no chirality: left and right configurations are related by a rotation no readout can separate. Two proximity spheres on the tip carry `HandL` and `HandR`, and the glue reads their **difference**: the nearer palm reads higher at any distance, so the sign needs no absolute threshold and no scale (a foreign palm well outside the hand still reads a large fraction at this radius, so no threshold on one reading could be placed). The margin the glue demands on the difference is a proximity floor: a palm has to be inside the sphere. Two palms reading alike, or none, take no latch. The differential is read once, at the latch, the one instant the tip is provably in that palm, and recorded by a driver into `Palm/Hand`; the gate spheres' filters are never shut and the spheres are never read again. On a remote the grabber's palm trails the synced tip by the IK delay during motion, so a rung that re-read a sphere this small after the latch, or a filter that shut it, would refuse or drop the grip on every fast swing.

**The axis sign is one finger.** A point plus a symmetric capsule reads identically rotated 180° about the tip's perpendicular offset, so the palm readout yields the axis as a line, and the eight-box machine holds an orientation only for continuity. The index finger is on the thumb side of the palm whatever the curl (measured at rest across thirty hands, and through every shipped gesture on one base), and its along-axis coordinate is the one component a curl does not move. Two proximity spheres tagged `FingerIndex` sit on the readout's two axis proxies, at the palm midpoint plus and minus the sensed axis, and their **difference** is the sign: exactly "which side of the midpoint plane the index's nearest point is on", independent of curl and hand size, where a single receiver against a threshold confounds side with distance. The readout writes the axis to both proxies, so the sign mux is two aim constraints under one rotation constraint and costs no arithmetic. The cue pair's filters are **never** shut: the pinky-side contact breaks during a curl on a third of the surveyed hands, and a latched contact that fully breaks cannot re-latch while filters are shut, which would leave a dead receiver reading an unconditionally correct sign.

## How it works

The glue controller is `grab-prop`'s cell, clip table replicated binding for binding, plus a latch, a confirm dwell and a receiver stow; `controller.yaml` has the states, this is why they are shaped so.

- **`Arrive` before `Acquire`.** The bone snaps to the hand grab point over a few frames, and a latch taken on the first grabbed frame keeps whatever palm was nearest the prop's old position. The dwell and the poll are separate states because an exit-time rung that also carries conditions re-tests them once per clip period, so a dwell and a per-frame poll cannot share a state.
- **The latch needs all eight boxes reading, not just a decisive hand differential.** Between grabs the box hosts are animated to identity rotation at the acquisition scale, so they coincide as one cage-aligned cube whose half-width is the gate radius and contains the gate sphere. The box readings are then not geometry but proof that every box has acquired the palm before its filter shuts: after a stow each receiver re-acquires over a few frames drawn per receiver, and a box whose filter shuts first never acquires, so a latch on the spheres alone spins the stow loop. The Latched entry driver records the hand, shuts the box filters on frame 0 and steps the hosts to the tetrahedral working cage, rotation and scale together, on frame 1.
- **`Settling` freezes for a fill while the pipeline primes.** The residual and half-length land a few frames after working scale, and the cue two stages later still, since the proxies must move and the contacts sample after the constraint solve. `Settled` then polls the residual, the half-length band, the lever floor and the cue differential every frame, branching into a `Confirm` state by recorded hand and sign, or to `Reacquire` at the timeout.
- **`Reacquire` is a receiver stow, because a reopened filter does not re-acquire.** Contact acquisition is an enter event: a latched palm that leaves a box while filters are shut and comes back before they reopen is rejected on the way in, and once inside there is no further entry for the reopened filter to admit, so a loop that merely timed out and reopened would hold that hand position-only for the rest of the grab (readings flat at zero for the whole window, in the emulator). A slow stow re-acquires a sender already inside (measured in-client), so every box loss between the latch and the carry, and the settle timeout, pass through it: all twelve receiver GameObjects off for the dwell `Disabled` holds, then `Acquire` with filters open. Both stowing states hold that dwell because a receiver switched off and on inside one frame with a sender inside is deaf for the session; `Disabled`'s entry driver also zeroes the twelve params so nothing stale gates the enable that follows. The carry states keep a direct loss path to `Acquire`: a hand that leaves a working-scale box and returns inside the one frame the reopen takes is not a hand.
- **`Confirm` is the temporal guard, because the sign is irreversible for the hold.** The held line runs a few degrees wrong for one to three frames at a fraction of a percent duty even inside a continuous hold, and the cue's own magnitude does not flag a wrong-line frame; deciding on one frame latches a 180°-wrong grip until the next grab. So every readout condition is re-tested each frame for the dwell, any one retreating past a looser bounce threshold returns to `Settled` (hysteresis: a reading dithering on its entry threshold cannot flap the two states), and the dwell's exit time is the decision. The dwell is not dead time: each `Confirm` state already drives the tentative grip, so the prop starts turning the frame the readout settles, and a bounce freezes it where it reached rather than snapping it back. In `Carry` neither the gate nor the cue is read again. A refusal is legible: the prop follows position and never turns.
- **Placement is the same idea with no constant to measure.** `Frame` sits at the tip with the sensed rotation and each grip node at its authored trim from it, so `Damped` carries a position smoother beside its rotation one, weighted toward the tip at home, frozen through acquisition and after a drop, toward `GripR` or `GripL` in carry. The rotation smoother is a self-sourcing damper (`spring-damping`'s pattern) chasing `Rotor`, smoothing the readout's pattern hops and, on remotes, the stepped hand. It sits beside `Rotor`, never under it: a child's world rotation follows its parent before the self-source reads it, and the damper then has nothing to lag. The origin is the grab point and not the sensed midpoint because the grab point is the client's and the same in both hands, while the midpoint carries each hand's capsule error.
- **Two palms in the core are rejected by residual, not identified.** Each box reads its nearest sender, so a second palm latched with the grabbing one usually keeps the residual high until the timeout stows; a left plus a right reading alike take no latch. Two same-handed palms, or two at unequal distances, are rejected only by the residual and the half-length band: a union that reads like one plausible capsule still engages, and narrowing the band toward the surveyed hands rejects more unions at the cost of large hands.
- **The working boxes are far larger than the palm on purpose.** On a remote the tip rides the synced grab point while the palm rides IK-smoothed bones about half a second behind, and must still lie inside every box's linear range. The boxes are still not the binding term: the gate spheres are the acquisition zone and the cue spheres are hand-sized, so a remote palm swinging past the cue's range before the sign is decided leaves the machine polling until the timeout stows.
- **The cage is tilted, cube diagonal to vertical, and the tilt is load-bearing.** A palm axis lying in one of the cage's coordinate planes has a mirror line about that plane's diagonal that reads identically on every box, a two-line ambiguity no residual can break. With a world-aligned cage, turning a palm-down hand about the vertical, or pronating with the forearm along a world axis, sits in that plane for the whole motion and the prop follows the wrong line for seconds. With every cage axis at the same elevation and 120° apart in azimuth, no horizontal or vertical motion stays in a cage plane; a crossing is transient and the hysteresis rides it out.
- **Every clip in the glue layer writes the layer's whole binding set,** `Frame` and `Rotor` weights included: on a scene binding a state writes only what its own clip writes, so a delta clip inherits the previous state's values.

## Before you compose it

Acquisition is the gate sphere alone; the bone length and the grab radius do not feed it. With `snapToHand` on the tip goes to the hand grab point wherever the hand closed, so the core only has to reach from that point to the far side of the grabbing palm, and the grab radius is a free reach knob. The box cube is sized from the gate radius and never binds first, so tightening bystander rejection is one radius.

Self senders are allowed on a hip-parked home, so the wearer's own resting hand often sits inside the acquisition core and another player's grab may engage only after it moves away; drag `HomeAnchor/Offset` out of the idle hand path if that bites. The wearer's own grab gets the full orientation path, per hand.

Keep `Container`, `Container/SourcePosition`, `Container/Rotor` and `GrabPosition` out of any re-parented subtree: a VRCFury clip binding through an MA-moved node is dropped at build (`nondestructive.md`). `FreezeRotation` must stay in the physbone's `ignoreTransforms`: it takes the whole tip subtree out of the chain, so the chain ends at `GrabBone_End` with no zero-length node behind it (one makes the grab solver assert every frame) and the readout rig is never enrolled as chain bones.

## Limits, stated

- At release each client freezes the pose its own readout held at its own release frame, so clients disagree about the resting rotation by however far the wrist turned over the IK-delay window. Per drop, never accumulating, erased by the next grab, absent for a release from a still hand. No client can tell it is the grabber's (the animator runs as the wearer's copy everywhere, and the grabber is in general a third player), so no dwell compensates.
- The roll channel depends on the client putting its grab point off the palm axis, which this entry neither controls nor measures. The lever floor reads midpoint distance, so it refuses a grab point on the axis near the palm's mid-plane and admits one displaced along the axis, which then rolls free.
- The line readout has attitudes two lines read alike; the cage tilt makes them transient under motion, not absent. A grab that starts still inside one can settle onto the wrong line and pass the residual gate, reproduced synthetically at a small grip offset and not seen in-game. Motion breaks it, and the sign latch rides the hop.
- The remaining error is the naive nearest-surface capsule model's slow pose-dependent bias, under a degree median and a few degrees at the tail: slight drift across the wrist's range, not shake.
- The authored grip lands a few degrees differently in each grabber's hand: the palm capsule is built from the avatar's rest pose and sits a dozen degrees off the anatomical width axis, differently per base. A constant per avatar, not drift.
- The chirality is the gate's, read once at the latch: a misread hand tag, or a bystander's palm nearer the grab point than the grabber's during the arrive window, shows as a mirrored grip on that client alone for the whole grab.
- Two co-located wearers of this entry reach the receiver-cluster count that reads wrong values.
- On a remote during fast motion an undecided sign drops to position-only and retries at the timeout cadence plus the stow dwell.
- A readout hop that reversed the held orientation mid-carry would leave a wrong latched sign for the rest of the grab; measured at zero occurrences inside a continuous hold, reasoned rather than measured in-client.
- Within roughly a kilometre of the world origin: the readout rides the client's float32 world-position error, negligible near the origin and a wandering bias of centimetres at 10 km, where nothing here works. A hand-edited hand collider breaking the SDK's automatic radius-to-height proportion gives that grabber a constant axis bias. The remote hand's readout noise is unmeasured; the client floor was measured on the wearer's own hand.

## Empirical constants (90 % rule)

Every value lives in `generate.py`; the table names the knob and the relation.

| Constant | Knob | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| Box geometry | `F`, `D` | face plane and depth at working scale; larger tolerates more remote hand lag, and the extents, lookup and every leaf clip follow from it |
| Sign margin | `MARGIN` | keep-previous hysteresis in residual metres; below it noise flips the held pattern, above it the pattern lags through degenerate regions |
| Settle gate | `RES_SETTLE`, `S_LO`, `S_HI` | residual and half-length band an engage requires; wider engages sooner, on worse geometry, and admits more two-palm unions |
| Square-root lookup | `LUT_LO`, `LUT_HI`, `LUT_N` | must cover the discriminant over the whole half-length band or S reads wrong while the gate still passes; the generator refuses a lookup that does not |
| Damping | `SMOOTH_W` | the non-self weight on both of `Damped`'s smoothers, mirrored in the prefab and pinned by `--check`; closes `w / (1 + w)` of the remaining way per frame, so lower is smoother and lags more |
| Grab radius, bone length | `GRAB_RADIUS`, `BONE_END` | the physbone reach and `GrabBone_End`'s local position, mirrored in the prefab and pinned by `--check`; neither sizes acquisition, and the bone stays short so the chain ends where the tip subtree leaves it |
| Settle timeout | `SETTLE_TIMEOUT` | seconds after the latch before the loop stows and re-acquires; also the longest stall after a `Confirm` bounce whose rung stays failed |
| Stow dwell | `DISABLED_DWELL` | seconds the receivers stay off in `Disabled` and `Reacquire`; must outlive one evaluation at the lowest frame rate, and every extra frame is re-acquisition delay |
| Arrive dwell | `ARRIVE_DWELL` | how long a fresh grab waits before the latch rungs arm; must outlast the bone's snap to the grab point, and every extra frame is latency on every grab |
| Gate radius | `GATE_R` | the two hand spheres' proximity radius: the acquisition zone and the hand differential's scale; the hosts' acquisition scale derives from it so the coincident cube always contains the sphere; smaller rejects more bystanders and admits fewer real grab points |
| Gate margin | `GATE_M` | how decisive the hand differential must be at the latch; below it two palms or none take no latch |
| Cue radius | `CUE_R` | the two index spheres' proximity radius; the argmax of the worst-case differential over the measured hands, smaller goes blind on large hands and larger flattens the differential |
| Cue margin | `CUE_M` | how decisive the sign differential must be; sized against the readout's own axis error on the measured hands, never against the emulator, which reads it low |
| Lever floor | `MM_MIN` | squared tip-to-midpoint distance below which the settle branch refuses |
| Fill | `SETTLE_FILL` | time frozen after the latch, sized so that at the frame-rate floor the fill plus the confirm dwell outlasts the readout pipeline, the cue's two extra stages included |
| Frame-rate floor | `FPS_FLOOR` | the frame rate the arrive dwell and the fill are frame counts at; a clip length is wall-clock, so a slower client fills fewer frames, which costs a bounce rather than a wrong latch while the confirm dwell still outlasts the pipeline there |
| Bounce hysteresis | `BOUNCE_H` | how far a reading must retreat past its entry threshold before `Confirm` bounces; 1 lets a dithering reading flap the two states, smaller lets a worse reading ride the dwell into the latch |
| Confirm dwell | `CONFIRM_DWELL` | how long every engage condition must hold before hand and sign latch; must exceed the readout's transient wrong-line runs at the frame-rate floor |
| Grip poses | `GRIP_R`, `GRIP_L`, `GRIP_R_POS`, `GRIP_L_POS` | the authored per-hand grip rotation and trim off the grab point, mirrored in the prefab nodes of the same names |

## Verifying the install

Enable on: the prop rests at `HomeAnchor/Offset` on the wearer's hips; at the origin means the BoneProxy never resolved. Grab it with one hand and hold still: the prop snaps to the grab point and within the confirm dwell turns to the authored grip and eases into the palm. A prop that follows position but never turns is a refusal: a second palm in the core, a hand outside the boxes, the index finger too far from the grip for the cue, a tip on the palm axis, or `FreezeRotation` missing from the physbone's ignore list. Roll the wrist: the prop rolls with it, always. Grab with the other hand: the other authored grip, not a mirror of the first. Drop and re-grab as `grab-prop`; repeated pickups land in the same grip.

`generate.py --check` asserts the prefab surface no compile reads: FullController order and the `globalParams` wildcard; each receiver's parameter mapping, tag, shape, size, locality and filters serialized open; the cage tilt and scale source; the constraint sources and zero offsets of `Rotor` and `Frame`, the aim pair's targets and up mode, the grip nodes' rotations, positions and absence of constraints; every constraint's source-list length against its filled slots (a slot past the length solves in the editor and not in the client); the absence of any capture or world-up fallback node; and the physbone's snap, dynamics, grab filter, radius, bone length and ignore list. `twin.py` is the per-frame reference the compiled readout is scored against, not a build input: `truth <sweep>` checks the midpoint and lever proxy against a recorded sweep, `compare` scores a Unity edit-tick dump against the twin's. It evaluates the palm half only; the recorded sweeps carry no finger.

The emulator cannot reproduce a snap-on grab (its grab helper applies the offset unrotated) or the client's finger capsule shape, and it has no contact noise. So the real grab point's offset, the sign cue against a real index at real curl and splay, the remote hand against the gate and cue spheres through the IK delay, the confirm dwell against contact noise, cross-client agreement over repeated pickups and late join all need two clients in-game. The checklist below is that run.

## In-game checklist

Two clients: A grabs, B watches; the last item needs a third player. A refusal is a prop that follows position and never turns.

- [ ] **Two-node chain.** Grab, carry, drop and re-grab, wearer and visitor: the snap lands in the hand and the release does not spring.
- [ ] **Grab and placement.** A grabs as wearer and as visitor at several wrist attitudes: every grab engages and the payload eases into the palm within the confirm dwell on both clients. Note how far the grab point sits from the palm; the gate radius and lever floor were sized without that read.
- [ ] **Sign never 180° wrong.** A holds still at several attitudes: on B the prop never presents rotated 180° about the palm axis, and any refusal engages within a couple of seconds of holding still.
- [ ] **Per-hand grip.** Left, drop, right: each hand lands its own authored grip, not a mirror, as seen by A and by B.
- [ ] **Repeated pickup and drop.** Five or more cycles at varied attitudes: no disagreement between A and B grows cycle to cycle, and whatever a drop leaves is gone at the next grab.
- [ ] **Fast wrist before release.** A releases mid-rotation: the frozen rest orientations differ by no more than the wrist turned over the sync delay; a still-hand drop afterwards leaves none.
- [ ] **Two-handed grab.** Both of A's hands on the tip: refusal; releasing one engages within the settle timeout plus the stow dwell.
- [ ] **Roll at a real grip.** A rolls the wrist a full turn: the prop rolls with it throughout, never spinning free or holding still.
- [ ] **Remote fast swing during acquisition.** A grabs and swings at once: on B the prop is position-only through the swing and engages once the hand settles.
- [ ] **Cue margin against real fingers.** With finger tracking, A splays the index hard toward the middle finger while gripping: engage or refuse, never the wrong sign.
- [ ] **Wearer's idle hand.** Home on the hips with the wearer's hand resting beside it, a visitor grabs: engages once the wearer's hand moves away, never with the wearer's palm as the grip.
- [ ] **Late join.** B joins after A dropped the prop: B sees it hidden until A's next grab, then carried and dropped where A sees it.
- [ ] **Bystander's hand.** A third player's hand, index included, near the held or dropped prop: sign, hand and pose never change on any client.

## Rig

`grab-prop`'s position cell with the readout rig, the gate, the cue and the grips added under the physbone tip; edit it in place, `Locked` on every constraint, source weights swapped by the clips.

    ├─ Container                      grab-prop's position cell, untouched
    │  ├─ Rotor                       VRCRotationConstraint ← [HomeAnchor/Offset, GripR, GripL]; disabled = the rotation freeze
    │  ├─ Damped                      VRCRotationConstraint ← [self, Rotor] and VRCPositionConstraint ← [self, Container, GripR, GripL]: the smoothers; a sibling of Rotor, never its child
    │  │  └─ Payload
    │  └─ SourcePosition
    ├─ HomeAnchor / Offset            MA BoneProxy → Hips; the home attitude is the Offset transform, never a baked offset
    ├─ GrabPosition ← [Offset, Container]

    │     └─ GrabBone_End             the chain's last node
    │        └─ FreezeRotation        VRCRotationConstraint FreezeToWorld, alone on its node: the readout frame is world-attitude
    │           ├─ DropPosition
    │           └─ Cage               VRCScaleConstraint → assets/World.prefab, alone on its node
    │              ├─ T1p T1m … T4p T4m   8 box receivers [Hand], local +Z = ±d_j; host scale and rotation animated: one coincident cube ↔ the tetrahedral cage

    │              │  ├─ ProxyB       localPosition = −axis (the same clips, negated)
    │              │  │  └─ CueN      sphere receiver [FingerIndex]
    │              │  ├─ UpAim        VRCAimConstraint +Y → Cage, WorldUp None
    │              │  │  ├─ Recon     VRCAimConstraint +Z → ProxyA, ObjectRotationUp against UpAim
    │              │  │  └─ ReconN    VRCAimConstraint +Z → ProxyB, ObjectRotationUp against UpAim
    │              └─ Frame           VRCRotationConstraint ← [Recon, ReconN], the sign mux; at the tip, so a grip node's position is a trim off the grab point
    │                 ├─ GripR        no constraint; the authored right-hand grip in its localRotation and localPosition
    │                 └─ GripL        no constraint; the authored left-hand grip, likewise
    ├─ FreezeToWorld
    └─ EditorOnly

`Rotor` carries the rotation channel so `Container`'s constraint and `GrabPosition`'s sources stay exactly `grab-prop`'s measured graph. The freeze is alone on `FreezeRotation` because a `FreezeToWorld` constraint co-located with any other writer of its transform wins silently, and the scale pin is alone on `Cage` so nothing animated competes with it; the collapse is animated on the eight hosts, uniformly, because tetrahedrally rotated boxes under non-uniform scale shear. `ReconN` sits under `UpAim` beside `Recon` so both read the up helper in the same solve group. Every rotation constraint is zeroed, never activated, and the grip lives in the grip node's own local rotation and position, never in a source offset: with identity offsets the carry pose is exactly `Frame · Grip`, which is the whole claim.
