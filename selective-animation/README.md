# selective-animation — per-player targeted animation (Structural Module)

An animation only the player you point at can see. A `VRCRaycast` in `HitPlayers` mode rides your forearm axis, and because a player capsule exists only on its own owner's client, the ray registers a hit on the targeted player's machine and on no other — so their copy of your avatar runs the animation while everyone else's does not. Per-player selectivity at **0 synced bits**: the wearer's FX consumes one native `_Hit` bool.

**Provenance:** generalized from VRLabs' MIT-licensed [`Selective-Animation`](https://github.com/VRLabs/Selective-Animation), which established the mechanism. Its rig predates the SDK's own raycast (it pins `com.vrchat.avatars ^3.7.0`) and reconstructs a hit flag out of geometry: two FinalIK `GrounderIK` raycasts, configured identically and offset along a shared axis, whose end transforms coincide only when both strike the same surface, with a contact sender/receiver pair on those ends turning that coincidence into an animator bool. Native `_Hit` reports the same fact directly, so the second ray, both contacts, the two constraints that carried the ends, the scale-immunizer sourcing a never-instantiated `World.prefab`, and the Final IK dependency (with its "testing in Unity is not possible" caveat) all drop out — 16 GameObjects, 4 constraints, 4 FinalIK components and 2 contacts become one component and one aim constraint. The aiming is not theirs: they have the installer rotate a target by eye, this derives direction from bone positions (below).

This is a **structural** Module: the behaviour lives in the prefab's components, not an animator, so the entry ships a prefab and **no `controller.yaml`** — the gate's compile pass skips it, though its prefab-integrity pass still checks the prefab imports with no missing scripts (`CONVENTIONS.md` §The gate). Behavioural correctness rests on the install check below.

## Interface

- **Params:** out only — `SelectiveAnimation_Hit` (bool), `SelectiveAnimation_Ratio` and `SelectiveAnimation_Distance` (float), from the raycast's `Parameter` prefix. Declare the ones you read in your FX as ordinary animator params; the component writes them per-client. **Never declare them synced.** A natively-driven param is re-driven every frame by the local component, so a synced copy is a wasted bit (`runtime.md` §PhysBones states the rule for the same class of param), and in the emulator a synced raycast param reads the wearer's replicated value on a clone rather than that clone's own ray (`docs/verify.md`) — which is the opposite of what this entry is for.
- **Seam:** none — nothing merges into any controller. Drop the prefab at the avatar root; two MA `BoneProxy` anchors resolve `RightLowerArm` and `RightHand` from their humanoid bone references, so there is nothing to place or aim by hand. Two consumer surfaces: the `_Hit` param, and `HitPoint`, the raycast's result transform, driven in world space every frame — constrain a marker or payload to it for an aim point.
- **Dependencies:** `com.vrchat.avatars` (`VRCRaycast`, `VRCAimConstraint`) and `nadena.dev.modular-avatar` (`BoneProxy`), both already package dependencies. `VRCRaycast` is a recent SDK addition and both frameworks gate their param-prefix rewriting behind a version define (VRCFury's `VRCSDK_HAS_VRCRAYCAST`, MA's `MA_VRCSDK3_AVATARS_3_10_3_OR_NEWER`) — on an older SDK the prefix will not travel through a rename.
- **Required assets:** none. No renderers, no materials, no menu.

## How it works

**Direction comes from bone positions, never bone rotations.** `Aim` is a child of the elbow anchor carrying a `VRCAimConstraint` that points its +Z at the hand anchor, so the ray runs along the forearm on any humanoid rig. This is the whole reason the entry can self-place where the ancestor cannot: bone *axes* differ between bases (roll conventions are per-author), so a rotation baked against one rig aims somewhere else on the next, while the *positions* of the elbow and hand are unambiguous everywhere. Both are required humanoid bones, so they always map — a finger axis would read more naturally but fingers are optional in the humanoid rig. Measured on a vendor rig: `Aim.forward` matched the elbow→hand vector to **0.000°**, with both anchors landing on their bones at zero offset. The ray therefore points where your forearm points, which with arms at rest is at the floor — this is a gimmick you aim by raising your arm.

**What the ray can hit, and what "selective" therefore means.** `HitPlayers` queries the `Player` and `PlayerLocal` layers (measured: mask `1536`). Each client holds exactly one player collider — its own — so on any given machine the ray can only ever hit that machine's own player, which is precisely why the hit lands on the target and nowhere else (`runtime.md` §Other load-bearing components owns the mechanism and the upstream bug it rests on). Two consequences worth designing around: the wearer's client never registers a hit on the person they are aiming at, and selectivity means *whoever the ray crosses on their own client* — several players standing along the ray are each selected on their own machine, so this targets a line, not a person.

**Your own capsule does not block it.** The ray starts at your elbow, inside your own player capsule. Measured in world mode against a stand-in capsule with a target beyond it: a ray originating inside a capsule passes through and reports the next collider (`2.9500` to a wall at 3.0 m with 0.05 m half-thickness), never the capsule it started in — the raw `Physics.Raycast` control from the same origin agreed. VRChat's real player capsule exists only in-game, so treat that as corroboration and confirm it with the solo check below.

**A result transform is mandatory.** A `VRCRaycast` with `ResultTransform` unset never registers with the raycast command list and never casts — no hit, no params, no error, no diagnostic. `HitPoint` exists for that reason as much as for its own usefulness, and it sits at the prefab root because a result transform must never be the ray's own transform or a parent of it (it would be writing its own frame every frame).

**Miss behaviour and scale, as shipped.** `SnapToEnd` means a miss reads `_Hit` false, `_Distance` at max, `_Ratio` at `1.0`, and puts `HitPoint` at origin + direction × max. The alternative, `DoNothing`, freezes `_Distance`/`_Ratio` at the last hit's values while `_Hit` goes false and leaves `HitPoint` riding its parent, so a consumer reading a distance without gating on `_Hit` reads a stale one; shipping `SnapToEnd` keeps that trap unarmed. `ApplyTransformScale` is off, so reach is absolute metres; turning it on multiplies max distance by the origin transform's Z scale, after which `_Distance` stays absolute world metres while `_Ratio` becomes scale-relative (`runtime.md` §Scale) — that split decides which channel a scale-aware consumer reads. Raycast properties are read **live** each frame, so a runtime write to any of them takes effect (measured by flipping collision mode mid-play), unlike physbone settings which bake at chain init.

| Knob | Where it is authored | What turning it does |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | the raycast's `Distance` | how far down the arm axis a player is still selectable, in absolute metres (`ApplyTransformScale` off) |
| Direction | the raycast's `raycastDirection`, local to `Aim` | re-aims within the forearm frame — `+Z` is along the arm; edit this rather than rotating a transform, since it is numerically checkable |
| Which arm | the two `BoneProxy` bone references | swap both to `LeftLowerArm`/`LeftHand` for the left arm; anything else humanoid works the same way |
| Hit-point rotation | the raycast's `ApplyRotation` + `AlignmentAxis` | off by default (position only); on, `HitPoint` also orients to the surface, for a decal or a marker that should lie flat |

## Verifying the install

In play, the two anchors must sit **on** the elbow and hand bones; finding them at the avatar-root origin — the wearer's feet — normally means a `BoneProxy` never resolved. One editor-specific caveat before concluding that: in an unfocused or agent-driven editor MA's edit-time placement may simply not have ticked yet, which reads identically. Check the proxy's resolved `target` as well as its position; a resolved target with a stale position is the venue, not the rig.

`_Hit` will read false in the editor no matter what you do, because the editor has no player colliders at all and faking a capsule is a **false pass** — the client strips every collider off a real avatar (`docs/verify.md`). To prove the rig instead, flip `RaycastCollisionMode` to `HitWorlds` in play, put a collider a known distance along the arm axis, and read `_Distance` and `HitPoint`: measured `0.7448` against a target 1.0 m out with a 0.25 m half-extent, on the axis the aim constraint produced. Flip it back afterwards.

Per-player behaviour is in-game only, and it splits into two checks of very different cost. **Solo**, one client: whether your own ray self-registers on your own capsule — raise your arm so the ray crosses your own body and watch `_Hit`. **Two clients**: selectivity itself, which no single-client venue can show.
