# contact-radar — per-sender contact slots with an exact burst origin (Module)

A particle burst wherever another player's right hand enters a sphere around you: several hands at once, each burst on its own hand, no retrigger for a hand dithering at the edge, and everything sensed per client so the burst lands on the hand as each observer sees it. Nothing here syncs but the enable — every copy of the avatar runs its own sensing, which is the only way the burst can sit on the hand where that observer's client draws it. **One synced bit; 4K + 1 receivers per copy** (5K + 1 with `fourBox`).

Two modes, one generator flag, one rig. **Dwell** (the root prefab) holds a hand from cube entry to cube exit and re-bursts each time it comes back inside the burst radius after retreating past the re-arm radius — poke, pull back, poke again — with the payload on the whole time the hand is inside the sphere, so a mesh in it rides the hand. **Entry** (`entry-mode/`) releases the slot a few frames after the burst (the burst state's own dwell, set by its Direct tree's data-dependent length), so a hand has to leave the acquisition cube and come back for another; a hand that stays in fires once. Pick dwell for a reactive bubble, entry for a doorbell. Ships at a 1 m sphere with 0.10 m of hysteresis in both modes; `Cage/Size` rescales the whole thing in the inspector (§Knobs). The zone can be drawn (§Ground truth). Hands already inside when the module comes up are not missed: at enable, at load and after a distance-hide the cube sweeps outward over a couple of seconds and acquires each one alone as the front reaches it — loud from the toggle (a puff per hand, the shield coming up), silent from a load or a resume (the shield was always running).

**Provenance:** `box-tracker`'s face-proximity readout (VRLabs Contact-Tracker ancestry) with the fourth box removed and the sender radius supplied by config; the buffer-particle form is hfcRed/VRLabs Particle-Bufferer (MIT); the tilted cage is `absolute-grip-prop`'s, kept here for a different reason (§Ground truth); the per-sender slot protocol — arm shut, open one at a time, hand off in the same evaluation, re-arm by scale collapse, latch on the rising edge of the slot's own Constant receiver a step ahead of the readings, and release a slot that has just latched a sender another slot already holds — is this entry's own, resting on the emulator measurements in §How it works.

## Ground truth

- **Seam:** VRCFury `FullController` on the prefab root, FX, `basis: mount-root`; the hierarchy names are the clip paths, so a rename under `Cage` silently unbinds a slot. `HomeAnchor` is an MA `BoneProxy` to Hips, referenced only as a constraint source — no clip path runs through it. `globalParams` is exactly `[ContactRadar/Enable]`; every internal name sits under `CR/` and instance-prefixes at build. Depends on the VRC SDK, VRCFury and Modular Avatar.
- **The menu front is one VRCFury `Toggle` on `ContactRadar/Enable`, synced, unsaved, default on.** Off stows the receivers and zeroes their floats on every client; on starts a loud sweep, so a hand already inside when you switch on gets its own slot and its burst as the front reaches it. **One instance per avatar** — the bare Toggle exports an unprefixed name, and two instances would collide on it.
- **Three ways in, one sweep, two voices.** The toggle (Disabled → Armed) sweeps loud. A fresh animator — a load, a late joiner's copy, a manual hide/show, a mirror clone — starts in `Boot` and sweeps silent: a hand found inside the sphere gets the marker and no puff in dwell mode, and is released without a burst in entry mode. A distance-hide fires `IsAnimatorEnabled` false one frame before it halts the animator (`docs/runtime.md` §Parameters carries the citation); every state parks in `Paused` on that frame, which collapses the boxes, and the resume passes through a collapse step before the silent sweep, so whatever the receivers did during the pause is discarded and the hands present at resume are re-acquired from scratch. Silent ends with the sweep: a hand that crosses in afterwards bursts loud.
- **View cull is the installer's to close, with renderer bounds.** A view-culled copy's animator stops with no signal, so a hand that entered while an observer looked away would burst late when they look back. An observer whose camera is inside any of your renderers' bounds never view-culls you, so set the avatar body's SkinnedMeshRenderer bounds to cover the working volume plus a toucher's reach — a 3 m half-extent around the hips at `Cage/Size` 1, more if you scale the knob up — and every toucher, whose head is an arm from their hand, keeps your animator running whichever way they face. This entry ships no renderer for it: a bounds-only mesh costs a renderer and a material slot on every copy for what one field on the body already does. An observer far away and looking away is the accepted residual; compose `anti-cull` to close that too.
- **Receivers are `localOnly: 0` and `allowSelf: 0` by necessity.** Remote copies must sense so the burst reproduces on every client; your own hand is excluded so a wearer's own gestures never fire it. Tag `HandR` is the config's; retag for another sender.
- **`Cage` is pinned to world rotation and world scale** by two constraints sourcing `assets/World.prefab`, a never-instantiated asset that resolves as world origin on every client, and rides the Hips by position only. World rotation keeps the cube's corners from sweeping a parked hand out and back in when you turn; world scale keeps every radius in absolute metres under avatar scaling. Do not instantiate or delete `World.prefab`, and never Activate either pin — their offsets must stay zero.
- **The acquisition cube is tilted, its body diagonal vertical, and the tilt is load-bearing.** A standing player's senders stack in a vertical column — head, mouth, hips, hands — and a world-aligned cube's vertical face admits the whole column in one collision step: the Open slot latches them all as one reading (their per-axis maximum, a phantom) and every other slot rejects them for as long as they stay. With every face at the same elevation the column meets a face at depths staggered by height, so each member arrives alone and takes its own slot; only members close in height, or a column approaching along a slice vertex of the tilted cube, still arrive together. Each `Slot<k>` carries the rotation and its `Output` the inverse, so everything a consumer hangs under `Output` stays world-aligned; the receivers are children of `Boxes` and read in the slot frame, and the sphere is invariant under it. The rotation is a named constant in `generate.py`, copied by hand into every owned copy — `--check` holds it here and `check_prefab` holds it on a copy.
- **Every slot carries one more coincident box, `Hit`, and the latch is that box's rising edge.** `Hit` is a Constant receiver on the same animated flag as the readout boxes, so it is admitted and rejected with them, and its value goes to 1 one collision step before a face-proximity value exists for the same overlap. That makes it the slot's own record of what *this slot* took, where a shared receiver can only report that something somewhere arrived. A layer that writes last frame's `Hit` back out as a second parameter turns the pair into a rising edge one animator frame wide at every frame rate; the slot's own readings stay behind it as the fallback rung. Two arriving hands merge only if they arrive within one collision step, not the two a proximity reading costs.
- **`Gate`, the shared OnEnter box, is still on the prefab and nothing reads it.** It is kept so the loss watchdog this entry does not yet ship needs no prefab pass, and its parameter stays declared in the document — a receiver parameter the document does not declare is not instance-prefixed at build, and the receiver then writes a name nothing reads. Leave the node alone; do not wire anything to it expecting the pre-`Hit` behaviour.
- **The sweep's slot-to-slot handoff leaves no gap in the cage.** The cube that reappears shut, to re-reject what the departing slot just took, appears at exactly the size that slot's flag-up cube last had while it could still admit — so no shell of the cage is ever offered by no cube at all, and a hand resting in that shell during a sweep is acquired rather than silently rejected by every slot. §How it works has the two halves that buy it.
- **The boundary is opt-in, and the controller draws it.** `Cage/Size/Boundary` ships inactive holding one unit mesh, `Sphere`, on the built-in default material. The Sweep layer writes its scale from CONFIG in every state and enables its renderer only while the toggle is on, so what is drawn is the burst surface by construction — never the re-arm band — and a radius change needs no prefab edit; only the saved renderer enable, which `--check` holds, keeps the edit-mode preview honest. The mesh is single-sided with outward normals and carries no UVs, so the built-in default material shows nothing from inside — the swap wants a `Cull Off` unlit (`overlay-shaders`). Activate the node and swap the material to use it; an optimizer that strips inactive objects removes it when you do not, which is the intended cost of leaving it off.
- **Distance culling is the one pause the rig handles by signal; view culling is the bounds requirement above.** A client that has distance-culled you cannot see the burst either; what matters is that its copy comes back correct, and the `Paused` path makes it.

## Knobs

One knob is a transform; the rest live in `generate.py`'s CONFIG and the prefab — regenerate, recompile `built/` as a unit, and the README stays as it is.

| Knob | Direction |
|---|---|
| `Cage/Size` (transform scale, on your instance) | the whole sensor at once — cubes, readout, sphere, both bands — since every radius is metres in this node's frame, and it ships at uniform 1. Hold it uniform, between 0.5 and 2.0: the one term that does not scale is the sender-radius bias, so the burst origin lands `senderRadius × (scale − 1)` off the hand per axis, inward when smaller and outward when larger (emulator-measured at 0.5 and 2.0: 2.5 cm and 5 cm per axis, inside the 2 s puff's own spread). Past that range, or for an exact origin at any other size, retune CONFIG and regenerate instead; `fourBox` removes the bias term outright, so the origin is exact at any scale |
| `K` | slots = simultaneous hands before a newcomer is missed until it re-enters the cube; each slot is one FX layer and four receivers on every copy (five with `fourBox`), plus the one retained gate, and the receiver cluster bug (`docs/runtime.md` §Contacts) is reached when two wearers stand together at high K. K also sets the dedup cost: each slot's post-latch state carries one release rung per other slot, and the always-live difference tree grows with K × the axis count. Other K: duplicate a `Slot<k>` subtree under `Cage` (receiver parameters `CR/Slot<k>/…`), set CONFIG, regenerate |
| `burstRadius`, `rearmRadius` | the sphere and the dwell band; the band must exceed the client's temporal noise on the readout at the world's working distance (`docs/runtime.md` §Contacts) or a parked hand dithers a burst. The band is the only re-arm dwell mode has; entry mode's is the cube face below |
| `acqHalf`, `holdHalf` | the acquisition cube a hand must enter and the larger hold cube it tracks in. Each face sits `acqHalf − burstRadius − senderRadius` outside the sphere along its own axis, and that gap is two things at once: the head start the four-frame pipeline gets before a fast hand is inside the sphere (it only decides how far inside a swipe bursts), and in entry mode the re-arm surface — a hand must retract past the face to burst again, so it is entry's hysteresis, and since the faces are tilted the retract distance depends on direction: a little further sideways, nearly twice as far straight up or down. Both ship small, because `acqHalf` is also what admits a hand that was never going to poke you: a bigger acquisition cube fills K faster. `holdHalf − acqHalf` is the admission hysteresis, the drift a held hand gets before it releases |
| `senderRadius` | the constant bias of the hand sender's nearest surface toward each face; measure it on a real capsule hand in-client and retune here, or re-derive it per sender from a fourth `X−` box exactly as `box-tracker` does (`fourBox`, below) |
| `fourBox` | measure the sender's radius from an opposed pair of boxes instead of assuming `senderRadius`, so the burst origin is exact for a hand of any radius and at any `Cage/Size`. It wants a rig the shipped prefab does not carry, and ships off: add an `X−` beside each slot's `X+ Y+ Z+` — duplicate the `X+` node, turn it 180° about Y so its +Z face is the cage's −X face, and point its parameter at `CR/Slot<k>/X-` — and `--check` refuses every slot that lacks one, through the entry door as well as the base. It costs one more receiver on every copy — 5K + 1 instead of 4K + 1, reaching the cluster bug (§Traps) at a lower K — and one more axis in the dedup comparison, which is right: two senders of different radii can agree on three faces and differ only on the fourth. The measurement is exported per slot as the AAP `CR/Slot<k>/R`, metres in the `Size` frame, for a consumer that needs the radius itself; `senderRadius` then reaches nothing but the reachability lints, which still size the cube against it as the assumed maximum |
| `stepSeconds` | every dwell that must span collision steps. The floor is not about slow frames: at or below 60 fps every frame carries a step, and a hitch only makes frames longer. It guards the fast end, where a step lands on only some frames and a dwell shorter than the longest gap between two of them can open and close with none sampling it. The lint holds it above that gap |
| `latchSeconds` | how long a slot that has latched waits for its own readings before giving the slot back. It must outlast one client step, because the edge fires a step before any reading exists; the lint holds it to a multiple of `stepSeconds`. Raising it costs a slot for longer on every admission the readings never complete, lowering it drops real admissions on a jittery client. It is not what survives a hitch longer than the whole wait — the rung order inside the state is, and the generator says so where a later edit would see it |
| `dedupEpsilon` | how close two slots' raw readings must be, on every axis, for a slot that has just latched to decide it is holding a hand another slot already holds. It is a band on the reading, not a distance, so the ball it describes scales with `holdHalf`; the lint refuses a ball as large as a sender, and refuses zero, since a float transition can compare greater or less but never equal. Too small and the client's readout jitter reopens the duplicate; too large and two genuinely distinct senders collapse onto one slot |
| `sweepSeconds` | the front's travel time from the cage centre to just past the acquisition face, on every sweep. Two hands whose Chebyshev radii in the tilted frame differ by less than one frame of front travel co-latch, so a slower sweep separates closer hands and a faster one looks snappier, and a low frame rate widens the window because the front moves per frame. The lint refuses a front that crosses more than a quarter of the cube per collision step |
| `lookupSegments` | the x² table resolution; the chord error is one-sided and inward, so a coarse table shrinks the effective burst radius slightly |
| `enableDefault` | the enable parameter's default in the emitted document; False for a module that should load quiet. The enable is `saved: false`, so every fresh animator takes this default — at False both Boot states fall straight through to `Disabled` without sweeping, and the first sweep is the toggle's own loud one. The VRCFury `Toggle`'s default on the prefab is the same bit from the other side and must agree; the shipped prefab has it on |

## How it works

Receivers aggregate senders, strongest wins, so per-sender behaviour exists only through the latch: the allow flags are checked when an overlap begins and never again. Four facts measured in av3emu carry the whole protocol. **Rejection is sticky** — a sender whose overlap began while `allowOthers` was shut is absent from that receiver's collision set for as long as the overlap lasts, whatever the flag does later, on Proximity, Constant and OnEnter alike. **A scale collapse and restore is a fresh episode** for every sender present, with no deaf failure: too short for a step is a silent no-op, never a false zero (a GameObject bounce that short comes back deaf). **A same-evaluation flag handoff between two receivers partitions senders exactly**, and a sender acquired in the one step before the flip is double-admitted. **A Constant receiver's value is written when a sender is admitted and recomputed when one leaves, and nothing refreshes it between** — so it reads 1 one collision step after an overlap begins, a step ahead of a Proximity value, holds 1 while any admitted sender remains however still they sit, and returns to 0 only when the last of them leaves.

Each slot is one layer (`generate.py`'s docstring walks the states). What is not derivable from the document: the Open slot latches on its own `Hit` box's rising edge, or on its own readings a step later when the Hit box missed the admission, and the ring rule opens the next Armed slot in the same evaluation — the successor reads the latcher's `Open` flag one frame stale, so it sees it still open, and the same fresh edge. That handoff window is one animator frame with no retry, so the rung that matters when it is missed is the one that used to be the corner case: when nothing is Open, the lowest Armed slot opens itself on its clip's exit-time crossing, and that crossing is on the common path, bounding an Open-less avatar to one `stepSeconds`. A latch whose readings never come (a partial admission) recycles after `latchSeconds`, as `Partial` does on the other side of the latch; a hand read by only some of a slot's coincident boxes (it happens on the frame the slot opens) parks the slot in Partial for a step and then Recycles it, because an Open slot that stalls blocks every admission on the avatar.

The burst is arithmetic, not a receiver: the tree sums `x = 2h·X+ − h − r` per axis (under `fourBox` a fourth box measures `r`, and the constants cancel) into the AAPs and `Output`'s localPosition, three 1D lookups add x², y², z² into r², and the transition compares r² against the two radii — which is what makes the zone a sphere and the hysteresis per-sender, and what lets the whole readout live in the tilted slot frame without a correction.

Dedup is the same arithmetic used against the rig itself. The hold cube is larger than the acquisition cube, so a tracked hand that retreats into the shell between the two faces and returns is a *fresh* overlap for whichever slot is Open, and a fast entry whose position jitters across a face can be admitted two or three times over; nothing in a per-slot latch can refuse that from its own bookkeeping. So one always-live layer publishes, for every pair of slots and every axis, the difference of their raw box readings — built from the raw readings and not the signed readout, because a blend weight cannot be negative — and a slot in the state reached only from `Latch` releases itself when every axis of that difference is inside `dedupEpsilon` against a slot already holding a sender. Two coincident congruent boxes compute one sender's value identically, so the difference is a true zero rather than a small one. A slot already inside the sphere wins outright; between two slots still in the shell the lower index wins, and the rungs are asymmetric precisely so the two can never release each other. A hand that re-arms inside the band re-enters a twin state that carries no dedup rungs, so the comparison runs on the frames after an admission and never again for the life of a track.

The front is the same protocol run outward from the centre. While the shared `CR/Sweeping` flag is up every Armed slot sits collapsed, so it holds no rejections, and the one slot in `Sweep` rides its cube on the shared `CR/Sweep` front with the flag up, the gate riding the same front a frame behind it: a hand's overlap begins when the growing face reaches its surface, so each is admitted alone. On the latch the ring opens the next slot into `SweepShut` — its cube appears shut for a step, re-rejecting the hand just latched and anything else inside the front (the Recycle idiom) — and closing the gap between the two cubes takes both halves of one fix. The `Sweep` layer takes the same rung the slot does, the sweeping slot's own admission edge, so the front freezes on the frame the slot latches instead of a frame after its `Front` flag drops; and the shut cube scales off a second AAP holding *last* frame's front, so it appears at the size that just admitted rather than at a front that has already moved on. A hand exactly at that size was admitted by the slot that just latched and is correctly re-rejected by the coincident shut cube; a hand the front has not reached is picked up by the successor's flag-up cube on the next frame. The freeze needs the edge, so a handoff whose `Hit` box was missed still costs the two frames of gap this replaces. The sweep ends when the front passes the acquisition face: that slot goes `Open` at full size, the `Sweep` layer goes `Idle`, and the Armed slots restore shut with nothing unlatched left inside. The five shared AAPs (`Sweeping`, `Silent`, `Sweep`, `SweepBase`, `SweepPrev`) have exactly one writer, the `Sweep` layer, whose every state writes all five — a WD-ON blend-tree state does not hold an AAP it leaves unwritten (measured: it reverts to the default on the next frame), so a value that must persist is written back to itself through a direct child weighted by its own value. A transform the same tree scales by an AAP needs a weight-1 partner on the same property: a Direct tree whose weights on a transform curve sum below 1 blends the remainder toward the node's rest value (measured on the gate, which then snapped to its rest size on the enable frame instead of riding the front out), so the gate and the slot boxes each pair their `× Sweep` clip with a collapsed constant one. Slot layers read them and report their own `Front` flag for the layer to ramp on. `Silent` picks the endpoint for a hand the front finds inside the sphere: `TrackInSilent` in dwell mode holds `Payload` on with the buffer particle's own GameObject off, so the marker shows and nothing is born; entry mode releases straight to `Recycle`.

The burst states toggle one node, `Payload`, on while the hand is inside the sphere, and it serves two consumers at once because they read it differently: `Burst` inside it is a renderer-less buffer particle born at `Output` on the frame the wrapper enables (the edge — one puff per entry, however long the hand stays), and a mesh inside it is visible for as long as the wrapper is on (the level — the dwell demo's `Marker` rides the hand until it retreats past the re-arm radius). `Emit`, the visible burst, is `Burst`'s Birth sub-emitter and sits outside the wrapper, directly under `Output`, never disabled — disabling it is what truncates a burst in flight — and its sub-emitter reference is a non-child link Unity's inspector will not author; if a re-edit drops it, the buffer fires and nothing visible emits.

## Traps

- **The co-latch window.** A second hand whose overlap began within one collision step of the first is admitted by the same slot and rejected by the next for its whole overlap. The readout is then a per-axis *maximum* over the two hands — a corner that is never nearer the centre than either hand on same-sign approaches (it delays or suppresses a burst) and can sit inside the sphere when neither hand does only when the hands approach on opposite signs of different axes: one burst at a phantom point, and the second hand invisible until it exits the cube and re-enters. The `Hit` box is what makes the window one step and not the two a Proximity reading costs; same-step entries are one admission and merge regardless, and dedup has nothing to compare — one slot, one reading.
- **A column still merges its members that are close in height, and at some approach directions.** The tilt staggers arrivals by height difference, so two senders a few centimetres apart vertically — a hand beside a hip — still cross together, and a column approaching along the direction of a slice vertex of the tilted cube meets two faces at once and merges the pair that straddles the vertex height. Measured on a synthetic four-sender column: every member on its own slot at most azimuths, the two lowest merged at the vertex azimuths, and the one-collision-step admission window recovered those at high frame rate.
- **Two hands at one Chebyshev radius co-latch on a sweep.** Radii in the tilted frame: the front reaches both within one frame of its own travel, so the sweeping slot admits both and reads their per-axis maximum. `sweepSeconds` and the frame time are the two knobs on that resolution; the co-latch window above is the same mechanism.
- **Exhaustion mid-sweep leaves `Sweeping` up.** With every slot latched nothing sweeps and nothing clears the flag; the next recycled slot resumes the front from where it stopped and finishes the sweep — silently, if the sweep was — so a hand that arrives during that gap and is found by the resumed front may take the silent endpoint. K hands present at enable is the case.
- **A view-culled observer still misses what happened while they looked away**, and inflated body bounds only guarantee that an observer *inside* them never view-culls you. A far observer who turns back sees the state the pause left, bursts included; that is the declared residual, and `anti-cull` is the remedy.
- **The boundary can hide a hand that still bursts.** The drawn surface is the burst surface; the re-arm band lies outside it, so a hand that has retreated a little past the mesh still holds the payload until it clears the band.
- **The burst trails the hand by about four frames of travel.** The readout lands one frame behind the hand and the r² predicate three more (two collision steps for a Proximity value, one AAP hop, one transition read), so the burst fires inside the sphere by the hand's speed times four frames — a few centimetres for a slow poke, tens of centimetres for a swipe — and `Output` on the burst frame sits one frame of travel behind the hand. Widen `burstRadius` if fast hands matter.
- **A hand that stays inside holds its slot** (dwell): a friend standing with a hand in your cube occupies one of K until they move it out of the hold cube. Entry mode releases at the burst instead.
- **Exhaustion.** The Open slot fires with no Armed slot left: nothing opens, and a hand entering meanwhile is rejected by every slot until it leaves the cube and re-enters. In dwell mode that is K hands inside the cube; in entry mode it is K hands loitering in the shell between the sphere and the cube face. No preemption ships: evicting a tracked hand to serve a newcomer would cost a victim ladder per slot and still miss the hand it evicted.
- **Every step-spanning dwell is authored in seconds, never frames**, and every slot state writes every flag, the payload toggle and the buffer toggle, zeros included — a state that wrote only its own 1 would leave `Open` stuck and the ring rule firing forever. The generator enforces both; a hand edit of `controller.yaml` is how they break.
- **The readout coefficients and the hold cube are one unit.** `holdHalf` is baked into the readout clips, and `senderRadius` with it unless `fourBox` measures it; resize the boxes only through CONFIG.
- **A stow leaves a slot deaf to its own edge until its box next sees a sender leave.** A receiver whose GameObject is stowed reads 0, and on re-enable comes back reading the value it held at stow with nothing in its collision set. For a `Hit` box that is a rise, and it lands on the slot's first Armed frame — where no rung reads that slot's own edge, and every ring rung also requires the slot's `Open` flag, which Armed writes 0. So the false edge is harmless where it falls. The cost is the other direction: while that stale value stands, the next *genuine* admission raises no edge at all, and the slot latches a step later on the readings rung and hands the ring on a step later, with the sweep's front-freeze not firing for that one handoff. It clears the first time that box sees a sender leave. A load or a toggle-on in a crowd is the case, and the degradation is back to the pre-`Hit` timing, not a loss.
- **Dedup catches the duplicate admission the shell between the two cube sizes creates, and three cases defeat it.** That shell is the point: a tracked hand retreating into it and coming back is a genuinely fresh overlap for the Open slot, which is why duplicates exist at all, and setting `holdHalf` equal to `acqHalf` removes the shell at the cost of the hold hysteresis. What dedup will not catch: two hands admitted in the same step (the co-latch above — one slot, one reading); a duplicate admitted alongside a *second* sender in the same step, since the new slot then reads the pair's maximum and the old slot reads one hand, and the difference is not zero; and two genuinely distinct senders that sit within `dedupEpsilon` on every axis while one of them is newly admitted, which releases a real track. A consumer reading a slot's readout per slot also sees a one-frame identity pop when a fresh lower-index slot displaces a fresh higher-index one — the burst is unaffected, one hand still gives one puff.
- **4K + 1 coincident receivers on every copy, 5K + 1 under `fourBox`.** Two wearers side by side approach the ~24-receiver cluster bug as K grows; K is the knob, `fourBox` adds one more per slot, and off stows them all.
- **Nothing here is faithful in-client until measured there:** the emulator's readings are bit-constant, the client's dither with distance from the world origin, and the client refreshes receivers in only a fraction of frames. The dwell band and the entry-mode cube margin are the remedies; size them from a two-client observation, not from the emulator.

Deliberately not shipped: a cylinder zone (it does not fit a tilted cube without a much larger one, and the height compare needs the frame undone in the tree), an `allowSelf` self-test variant (it changes what the pattern senses), one `Emit` shared across slots (a possible material-count optimization, untested), and preemption.

## Entry mode

`entry-mode/ContactRadarEntry.prefab` is a prefab **variant** of `ContactRadar.prefab` at its own CONFIG's lower K. It customises exactly three things, each stated here because a removal is unvalidated (`docs/nondestructive.md`): the inherited root `FullController` is removed and re-added pointing at `entry-mode/built/`; the slots above its K are removed as variant overrides; and each kept slot's `Marker` is removed, because here `Payload` is on only for the burst state's few frames and a mesh inside it would flash (keep one if a flash on poke is what you want). A silent sweep here releases a hand found inside the sphere without a burst — it then has to leave the cube to burst, entry's ordinary re-arm — and tracks a hand found in the shell, which bursts loud when it crosses in. `generate.py --check` names every removal. Every other node is inherited, so a retune of the shared rig reaches it without a diff. Its `built/` carries its own GUIDs.

## Verifying the install

Enable on, walk: `Cage` rides the Hips, world-aligned, at world scale 1 whatever the avatar's scale — finding it at the avatar root means the BoneProxy never resolved. Every `Slot<k>` reads the same rotation and its `Output` the inverse, so a `Marker` sits upright while its `Boxes` sit tilted; `Gate` reads the same rotation at the acquisition cube's size. Put a scripted `HandR` sender (`docs/emulator.md` §Fake another player's contact) straight in toward the centre: the Open slot's `CR/Slot<k>/Hit` goes to 1 one frame before that slot's own axis floats leave zero, the slot's flag shuts and its boxes expand on the frame `Hit` rises while the next slot's flag opens on that same frame, `Output` sits on the sender, and `Payload` enables as the sender crosses the burst radius with `Emit` emitting. Send four senders in as a vertical column: each takes its own slot in turn. Pull the sender past the re-arm radius and back: a second burst (dwell) or none until it leaves the cube (entry). Pull it out past the acquisition face but not past the hold face, then push it back in: still exactly one slot holds it — a second slot admits it, sees its own readings match the holder's on every axis, and releases itself within a frame or two of latching, with no burst from the second slot. In dwell the `Marker` cube rides the sender while it is inside the sphere and vanishes as it retreats past the re-arm radius; the slot itself is held until the sender leaves the hold cube. Scale `Cage/Size` on the instance and every distance above scales with it. Activate `Cage/Size/Boundary` and enter play: the sphere's renderer comes on with the toggle and reads its world size as twice the burst radius across. A second sender arriving while the first is inside gets its own slot and its own burst. Park three senders inside at different radii, switch Enable off and on: every `Boxes` collapses, the lowest slot's cube grows from the centre at about `acqHalf / sweepSeconds` (the ramp aims 5 % past the face so the exit fires before the clip ends), each sender is admitted as the front reaches its surface (nearest first) with `Gate`'s scale tracking the sweeping slot's `Boxes` the whole way — a `Gate` that snaps to the acquisition cube on the enable frame instead of riding the front has lost the weight-1 partner beside its `× Sweep` child — gets its own slot and — inside the sphere — its own puff, and the slot after the last one grows to the face and opens at full size while the remaining Armed slots restore. At each handoff the incoming slot's shut cube reads the same size the outgoing slot's cube had on its last admitting frame: a shut cube larger than that is the front-freeze or the previous-front AAP broken, and it opens a shell no cube offers. Spawn a non-local clone with the senders still inside: its slots sweep the same way but every `Payload` comes on with `Burst`'s GameObject off (marker, no puff). Set the local runtime's `IsAnimatorEnabled` false, disable its Animator a few frames later, move the senders, re-enable both: every `Boxes` read collapsed from the frame after the flag, and the resume re-acquires the senders now present, silently.

What stays in-client only: the burst landing on the toucher's hand as the toucher and a third observer each see it, the real capsule hand's `senderRadius`, threshold dither at range, and the cluster bug with two wearers together — this rig puts four receivers per slot plus the gate at one point, and an avatar also carrying an owned copy under its own prefix stacks both counts against a bug whose neighbourhood is a couple of dozen. Dedup adds one in-client assumption the emulator is structurally unable to support: the emulator's readings are bit-constant, so two coincident congruent boxes trivially agree, while the client's dither with distance from the world origin. Dedup needs that dither to be common-mode across receivers refreshed in the same frames — identical inputs quantising identically — and if it is not, `dedupEpsilon` fails open and the duplicate returns in exactly the crowded, far-from-origin instance it was built for. Measure it with two clients, not here.
