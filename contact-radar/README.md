# contact-radar (Module)

A per-hand tracker: latches onto each other player's hand that enters a zone around you, one slot per hand, and exposes where that hand is so you can attach anything to it. Sensing runs on every client, so an attached prop sits on the hand where that client draws it. The shipped payload is a particle burst on entry; the production consumers of this entry attach props to the tracked hand.

## How it works

- Box contacts are selectively enabled and latched to track a series of matching senders as they enter and leave the zone. Each tracked hand occupies one of K slots (K is how many hands can be tracked at once). A slot is a cube of box contacts whose size and `allowOthers` flag the animator controls. With the flag up the cube takes a hand it newly touches; with it shut, the cube ignores that hand until their overlap ends.
- The **front** is the face of a cube that grows from the centre to full size (the **acquisition cube**) over `sweepSeconds`, collapses, and grows again; each growth is a **pass**. One free slot (one holding no hand) at a time **rides** the front: its own cube is that growing cube, flag up. The riding cube takes a hand as the front reaches it, while the other free slots wait collapsed, so no flag-up cube ever appears around hands already inside it. A hand that arrives is taken on the next pass to reach it.
- When the riding slot takes a hand, it shuts its flag and expands to the larger **hold cube**, which keeps the hand while it stays inside. The next free slot takes over the front in the same frame, at the size the front had reached.
- One Constant-mode contact per slot tells that slot that *it* took the hand, where a shared receiver could only report that something arrived somewhere. It reads the hand one collision step (the contact system's update tick, once per frame at 60 fps and below) after the hand arrives, where a Proximity contact takes two. That halves the window in which a second hand can slip into a slot that has just taken one.
- Three face-proximity boxes per slot reconstruct the sender's position. A blend tree sums the squares into r², and an animator transition compares r² against two radii, so the zone is a sphere with per-hand hysteresis.
- The cube is tilted so its body diagonal is vertical. The growing front then reaches a standing player's senders (head, hips, hands, where the tags match several) one at a time instead of all in one collision step, so each usually takes its own slot (§Traps). The sphere is the one zone shape that reads the same under the tilt.
- Every pass, the front also reaches each held hand still inside the acquisition cube, and the riding slot takes it too. A dedup layer compares slots' raw readings and releases one of the two slots holding that hand. It also releases a slot whose one reading merges several hands other slots hold.
- Nothing is synced but the enable.

## Install guide

1. Know what a slot does. A hand keeps its slot as long as it stays near you (inside the hold cube), so a prop hung on the slot follows the hand for as long as it is there; the shipped burst re-fires each time the hand pulls back a short distance and comes in again. For a doorbell, read the payload's rising edge and ignore its level (§Design notes, what a consumer reads).
2. Drop an instance of `ContactRadar.prefab` at the avatar root. `HomeAnchor` snaps to the Hips on its own; drag its `Offset` child to move where the zone sits. Edits belong on the instance or an owned copy, never on the package asset.
3. Set the body SkinnedMeshRenderer's Bounds (its `localBounds`) to reach twice `acqHalf` plus an arm's length in every direction from the hips; the tilted cube's corners reach nearly that far straight up and down. VRChat pauses a copy's animator while its observer is not looking at you, and this rig gets no signal for that, so a hand that entered while an observer looked away would burst late. An observer whose camera is inside any of your renderers' bounds never pauses you; with the bounds set, every toucher keeps your animator running. A far observer looking away is the accepted residual; compose `anti-cull` to close that too.
4. Scale `Cage/Size` if the zone should be larger or smaller. Uniform scale only, shipped at 1; it scales the zone, the readout and the hysteresis together.
5. Build. The expression menu gains a `Contact Radar` toggle, on by default.

Depends on the VRC SDK, VRCFury and Modular Avatar.

## How to use

- `ContactRadar/Enable` is the one parameter: synced, unsaved, on by default. Off stows the receivers and zeroes them on every client.
- Hang your prop under a slot's `Output`; it is the reconstructed hand position, in metres, one frame behind the hand, and stays world-aligned. Every slot needs its own copy. `Output` is always active, so a prop under it never hides on its own. A free slot's `Output` sits at the cage centre, and once per pass the riding slot's `Output` jumps onto a hand another slot already holds for the frames until dedup releases it. A prop that must show only while a hand is inside the sphere belongs under `Payload`.
- A controller merged into this prefab's FullController (`gimmicks.md` §Packaging and interface) reads the same position from `CR/Slot<k>/x`, `y`, `z`, and the hand's squared distance from the centre from `r2`. While a slot has taken a hand but has no reading yet, `x` sits at `+holdHalf`.
- `Payload` under `Output` is the node the controller turns on while the hand is inside the sphere: put a mesh there and it shows while the hand is inside; a buffer particle there fires once on entry. `Marker` and `Burst` are the shipped examples of each.
- Replace `Marker` (a placeholder cube on the default material) with your mesh, or delete it.
- Restyle `Emit`, the visible burst, which ships on Unity's default particle sprite, or delete `Emit` and `Burst` together if you want no burst. Never disable `Emit` while keeping it; disabling it truncates a burst in flight.
- Activate `Boundary` to draw the sphere. Its `Sphere` ships on the default material, which is invisible from inside, so give it a `Cull Off` unlit (`overlay-shaders`). The controller scales it to the sphere's radius and shows it only while the toggle is on.
- To sense another sender than `HandR`, see §Changing it.

## Additional notes

- **One instance per avatar.** The menu control is a bare VRCFury `Toggle`, which exports an unprefixed name; two instances would collide on it.
- **Your own hands never fire it.** The wearer is excluded by design, so nothing happens when you test alone (§Verifying the install).
- **Do not rename anything under `Cage`.** The clip paths are the hierarchy names; a rename silently unbinds a slot.
- **Leave `assets/World.prefab` alone.** It is a never-instantiated asset that resolves as the world origin on every client, and two constraints on `Cage` source it. Do not instantiate it, delete it, or press Activate on those constraints: their offsets must stay zero.
- **The receivers count against your rank.** They cannot be local-only, since remote copies must sense for the tracking to reproduce, so all 4K of them count toward the SDK's contact-count statistic (`optimization.md`). At the shipped K they fill the Good allowance on their own, so `fourBox` or any other non-local contact on the avatar crosses to Medium, and the 2K particle systems cap the avatar at Medium independently; raising K pushes both.
- **`Cage/Size` far from 1 shifts the readout.** Everything in the rig is metres in that node's frame except the sender-radius bias, so `Output` lands up to `senderRadius × (scale − 1)` off the hand per axis. Measured in av3emu at half and double size, that offset stays inside the puff's own spread; beyond that range, turn on `fourBox` to remove the term or retune CONFIG (§Knobs).
- **An optimizer that strips inactive objects** removes `Boundary` when you leave it off, which is intended. `Payload` is also inactive at rest with `Marker` and `Burst` inside it; an optimizer that strips inactive subtrees takes both.

## Knobs

`Cage/Size` is the transform above. Everything else is `generate.py`'s CONFIG: edit, regenerate, recompile `built/` as a unit (§Changing it). Each row gives the direction and the relation; the value lives in the code (the shipped zone radius is `burstRadius` there), and a lint in `generate.py` refuses a value that breaks the relation.

Terms used below, beside the front, pass, acquisition cube and hold cube of §How it works. The acquisition cube is the front's full size: the front runs a little past its face, then collapses to start the next pass. The **re-arm band** is the ring between the burst radius and the re-arm radius. The **approach gap** is the space between the sphere and the acquisition cube's face. The **hold shell** is the space between the acquisition and hold cubes' faces, where a tracked hand may drift before it is released; the front reaches only its inner edge. A hand's **tilted radius** is its distance from the centre as the tilted cube measures it, the largest of its three distances along the cube's axes. A **phantom** is one slot reading two or more hands at once, their per-axis maximum.

| Knob | What it does |
|---|---|
| `K` | Hands tracked at once. Each slot costs one FX layer and four receivers on every copy (five with `fourBox`); the dedup parameters grow with K squared, and each slot's dedup rungs, the phantom rungs included, number K − 1 raised to its count of face-proximity boxes. Past a couple of dozen coincident receivers, measure the configuration in-client rather than trust either number (`runtime.md` §Contacts). Lint: at least two, since one slot has nothing to hand off to. Changing K also means changing the prefab (§Changing it) |
| `burstRadius`, `rearmRadius` | The sphere, and the outer edge of the re-arm band. The band must exceed the client's readout noise at the world's working distance (`runtime.md` §Contacts) or a parked hand dithers a burst. Lints: the re-arm radius above the burst radius, and both reachable inside the cube on axis after the sender radius is added |
| `acqHalf`, `holdHalf` | The acquisition and hold cubes' half-sizes. The approach gap on each axis is `acqHalf − burstRadius`, plus the sender's radius, since the overlap begins when the hand's surface reaches the front. A hand the front reaches there is held before it enters the sphere and fires on crossing it (§Traps has the other case). The cube is tilted, so that distance depends on direction: a little further sideways, nearly twice as far straight up or down. `holdHalf − acqHalf` is the hold shell's depth. Both ship small, because a bigger cube fills K with hands that were never going to poke you, and a bigger `acqHalf` at the same `sweepSeconds` moves the front faster. Lints: hold at least acquisition; the burst reachable; the box within the SDK's serialized limit |
| `senderRadius` | The constant bias of a capsule hand's nearest surface toward each face. Measure it on a real hand in-client, or let `fourBox` measure it per sender |
| `fourBox` | Measure the sender radius from an opposed pair of boxes instead of assuming it, so the burst origin is exact for any hand at any `Cage/Size`. Costs one receiver per slot, one more dedup axis and a higher power on the dedup rungs, which is right: two senders of different radii can agree on three faces and differ on the fourth. Exported per slot as `CR/Slot<k>/R`. Needs an `X−` box the shipped prefab does not carry (§Changing it) |
| `stepSeconds` | Every timed wait that must span collision steps, the front's restart at the face included. Lint: above the longest gap between two steps (`runtime.md` §Contacts) |
| `latchSeconds` | How long a slot that has latched waits for its own readings before giving the slot back. Lint: at least twice `stepSeconds`, since the readings land a client step after the admission edge. Raising it holds a slot longer on every admission whose readings never come; lowering it drops real admissions on a jittery client |
| `dedupEpsilon` | How close two readings must be on an axis for dedup to call them one hand: against one holder on every axis for a second admission, against a different holder per axis for a phantom (§Design notes). It covers the one collision step by which two slots' readings can be skewed on the admission frame, not readout noise, which is common to coincident receivers: too small and a skewed duplicate survives, too large and two real hands collapse onto one slot. It is a tolerance on the reading, so the volume it describes scales with `holdHalf`. Lints: refuse zero, since a float transition never compares equal, and refuse a volume as large as a sender |
| `sweepSeconds` | The front's travel time from the centre to the acquisition face. It sets both the period (how often the front offers itself to each hand) and the resolution (how close in tilted radius two hands can be before they share a slot). Slower: a newcomer waits longer and a quick tap is likelier to be missed, but two hands must be closer to share a slot. Faster: the reverse. The sharing window is the front's travel in one collision step, so a low frame rate widens it. Each held hand the front reaches pauses it for at least `stepSeconds` (a **stall**, §Design notes); `sweepSeconds` plus those stalls is the effective period. Lint: the front may cross no more than a quarter of `acqHalf` in one collision step at 60 fps; the lint sets no maximum on `sweepSeconds` |
| `lookupSegments` | The x² table resolution. The chord error is one-sided and inward, so a coarse table shrinks the effective burst radius slightly |
| `enableDefault` | The enable's default in the document; False loads with the rig off, so the first pass starts when the toggle turns on. The prefab `Toggle`'s default is the same bit from the other side and must agree; `--check` holds the pair |

**Provenance:** the three-box face-proximity readout is `box-tracker`'s (VRLabs Contact-Tracker ancestry), with the fourth box moved behind a flag and the sender radius supplied by config. The buffer-particle form is hfcRed/VRLabs Particle-Bufferer (MIT). The tilted cage is `absolute-grip-prop`'s, kept here for a different reason. The per-sender slot protocol, the sweeping front and the dedup layer with its phantom rungs are this entry's own.

## Design notes

The state-by-state walk is `generate.py`'s module docstring, at the place an edit would break it, and `controller.yaml` comments every state and the rungs whose reason is not in their conditions. This section states what the rig guarantees and why.

**What a consumer reads.** The readout writes `Output`'s localPosition and `CR/Slot<k>/x`, `y`, `z` in metres in the `Cage/Size` frame, and `r2` in metres squared; under `fourBox`, `CR/Slot<k>/R` is the measured sender radius. A slot that has latched but has no readings yet parks `x` at `+holdHalf`, which a consumer can read as "not yet tracking". `Payload` toggles on while the hand is inside the sphere and serves two consumers at once: a buffer particle inside it reads the enable edge (one puff per entry, however long the hand stays) and a mesh inside it reads the level. A second admission that dedup releases never turns `Payload` on, but for its few frames it raises `CR/Slot<k>/Held` and moves the readout like any fresh track. A consumer reading per slot sees those, and sees a hand change slots when a fresh lower-index slot displaces a fresh higher-index one.

The protocol rests on four contact-runtime facts in `runtime.md` §Contacts: a receiver aggregates its senders and the strongest wins; rejection at acquisition is sticky for the whole overlap; a host-scale collapse and restore is a fresh overlap episode with no deaf mode; a Constant receiver's value lands one collision step before a Proximity one. One more is this entry's own, measured in av3emu and not established in-client: a Constant receiver's value is written on admission and recomputed on departure, with nothing refreshing it between.

**Sticky rejection is why acquisition is a sweep.** A full-size cube with its allow flag shut rejects every hand already inside for the whole overlap, and a full-size cube with its flag up takes everything that appears inside it on one collision step as one reading. So free slots wait Armed, collapsed and shut, holding no rejections, while one cube grows flag-up to the acquisition face. There the front parks at zero for `stepSeconds`, long enough for a collision step to sample it at any frame rate; that collapses the riding cube, ending every overlap it was rejecting, and the next pass grows from the centre with the flag still up. Every hand inside the acquisition cube is offered to one flag-up cube once per pass, and a hand the front has not reached waits for it rather than being lost. `CR/Slot<k>/Open` reads 1 on the one slot holding the front position (shut at it, riding it, or in the one-step grace a partial admission gets).

**The front changes slots in one evaluation.** `Hit`, the Constant box, rides the same flag as its slot's readout boxes, so its rising edge is the slot's own record that it took something, a step before any reading exists. The riding slot latches on that edge, shutting its flag and expanding to the hold cube, and the next Armed slot in ring order takes the front in the same evaluation. The front freezes on that edge. The successor appears shut at the size the front had on the admitting frame for a whole step, re-rejecting everything inside it, the hand just taken included, then raises its flag and rides on from that size, so no radius goes unoffered. When the edge is missed the slot latches on its readings a step later, and that one handoff leaves a thin band that pass skips and the next offers. Release reads the axis readings alone and never `Hit`: an admission the `Hit` box missed tracks with `Hit` still zero, and a release rung on `Hit` would recycle it on its first tracking frame.

**A released slot goes straight back onto the front when every lower-index slot is tracking a hand.** Recycle collapses its boxes for one step, which ends every overlap it held; the slot then takes the front directly if nothing else is Armed or holding it, and otherwise waits Armed. The lower-index condition is the tie-break: flags are read a frame late, and two slots released on one frame would otherwise both take the front.

**The cage is pinned to the world.** `Cage` is held at world rotation and world scale by the two `World.prefab` constraints and rides the Hips by position only. World rotation keeps the cube's corners from sweeping a parked hand out and back in when you turn; world scale keeps every radius in absolute metres under avatar scaling. The rotation is the `TILT` constant in `generate.py`; `--check` holds it here and `check_prefab` holds it on an owned copy.

**A distance-hide discards the pause.** A distance-hide announces itself one frame early (`runtime.md` §Parameters and network sync); every slot parks and collapses its boxes on that frame, so whatever the receivers did during the pause is discarded and the hands present at resume are re-acquired from scratch. The dedup layer alone never parks, so its edge memory survives a resume. The Sweep layer is the sole writer of the front's parameters and writes all of them in every state (`runtime.md` §Animator evaluation says why). It also draws the boundary, which is why a radius change needs no prefab edit.

**Dedup releases the second slot on a hand.** Every pass re-admits each held hand the front reaches, and a hand that crosses the front back and forth can be admitted more than once in a pass; no per-slot bookkeeping can refuse either. So an always-live layer publishes the per-axis difference of every pair of slots' raw readings, raw rather than the signed readout because a blend weight cannot be negative, and a slot that has just latched releases itself when that difference is inside `dedupEpsilon` on every axis against a slot already holding a hand. A slot that has settled, inside the sphere or in the re-arm band after it, wins outright whatever its index; between two fresh slots still outside the sphere the lower index wins, and the rungs are asymmetric so neither can release the other. The comparison runs only while a slot is fresh: from its admission until its hand first reaches the sphere or, outside it, until a few frames pass with no rung fired, after which the slot settles where it is. So two real hands that drift close later are not collapsed, and a hand held outside the sphere is not re-taken from its holder by every lower-index rider the front sends past it. Each re-admission stalls the front: for the successor's shut step when another slot waits Armed to take the front, and for the whole cycle of readings, dedup frame, collapse and shut step when the rider was the last free slot, as with K − 1 hands held. The hand stays tracked throughout, by its holder, or by the rider when both are fresh and the rider's index is lower.

**The phantom rungs release a phantom of held hands.** Two held hands the front reaches on one collision step are latched together as one reading, which matches no single holder. A Proximity receiver reports its strongest sender and coincident congruent boxes read one sender bit-identically, so each axis of that reading equals one holder's reading on that axis. So a phantom rung releases a fresh reading that matches, axis by axis, readings held by at least two other slots, with the settled-holder rule applied per holder. The rungs cover every assignment of axes to holders rather than accept any two matching axes, because that looser test misses a phantom of three hands holding one axis each, and releases two real hands that share two coordinates on every pass.

## Traps

- **A same-step merge with an unheld hand in it keeps its slot until its last hand leaves the hold cube, or it is down to one hand another slot holds.** Two unheld hands the front reaches on one collision step, or a held hand and a newcomer, share the riding slot as a phantom; the window is the front's travel in one step (§Knobs, `sweepSeconds`). A teleport or respawn into a group, or several avatars loading beside you at once, is the common case: every sender already inside the riding cube merges on that step, and the front offers the rest one at a time. The readout is the per-axis maximum in the tilted frame, a corner that takes the farther hand's coordinate on an axis where both are positive and the nearer hand's where both are negative. So it can sit outside the sphere while a hand is inside, which delays or suppresses the burst, or inside it when neither hand is: one burst at a phantom point. The phantom rungs cannot release it, since no other slot held all its hands when it formed, and once its reading has reached the sphere nothing re-examines it. Later passes offer each hand again, and a hand taken that way can hold a slot of its own beside the merge. When a hand leaves, the point walks out with it, crosses the re-arm band, and the payload drops and re-fires once as the point snaps to the remaining hands; that repeats at every departure. The slot is released only when the last hand leaves, or by dedup once the one hand left to it is one another slot holds; until then a remaining hand with a slot of its own is tracked twice.
- **The tilt staggers a standing column; it does not separate every pair.** Where the tags match several of a player's senders, two of them still share a slot when their tilted radii fall within one step of front travel: members close in height, a hand beside a hip for instance, and a pair equally far above and below the height at which the growing cube first touches their column.
- **A new hand waits for the front, and a quick tap can be missed.** A hand is taken only when the front reaches it, up to one effective period after it enters (§Knobs, `sweepSeconds`), and one that enters and leaves within a pass can go unseen. The wait is longest with K − 1 hands held, since each adds its stall, and with every slot holding a hand nothing rides: a newcomer waits until a slot frees, then up to one effective period more. A hand first reached inside the sphere fires the payload where it is found, which can be deep inside. A hand already held fires as it crosses into the sphere, late by the frames the readout and r² take, so a fast hand fires inside the sphere; widen `burstRadius` if that matters.
- **Dedup's blind spots.** Besides the merge above, dedup and the phantom rungs release a real hand whose reading matches other slots' readings on every axis within `dedupEpsilon`, whether one holder's or a different holder's per axis; the front re-offers it every pass and it is released every pass while that holds.
- **Every hand already inside fires the payload again at each load, mirror clone, manual hide and show, and distance-hide resume.** The front re-acquires them from scratch and cannot tell a resident from an arrival, so an edge reader (the shipped burst, a ping) fires once per resident at each of those events while a level reader (a marker, a mosaic) simply comes back on. Distance-hide resumes are frequent in a busy instance; a consumer whose edge payload must not repeat needs its own memory of who was already there. On a late joiner's client the rig may not sense players who were already in the instance at all (`runtime.md` §Contacts).
- **A hand within a sender radius of the cage centre is never taken.** A collapsed cube still has a centre, and a hand whose surface overlaps it keeps that overlap through every collapse and restart, so no fresh episode ever begins for it. The shipped cage sits at the hips, so that volume is inside the wearer's own body.
- **The boundary can hide a hand that still holds the payload**: the drawn surface is the burst surface, and the re-arm band lies outside it.
- **The readout coefficients and the hold cube are one unit.** `holdHalf` and `senderRadius` are baked into the readout clips; resize the boxes only through CONFIG, never on the prefab.

Deliberately not shipped: a cylinder zone (it needs the tilt undone in the tree, and a much larger cube); an `allowSelf` self-test variant (it changes what the pattern senses); one `Emit` shared across slots (a possible material-count saving, untested); a standing acquisition face beside the front to catch the taps it misses, since the rig is built to track hands that stay; a quiet mode for residents re-found at a load or resume (`generate.py`'s docstring has the trade); and preemption, since evicting a tracked hand for a newcomer would cost a victim ladder per slot and still miss the hand it evicted. Re-examining a settled slot, the one way to release a merge found inside the sphere, is preemption by another name.

## What is not proven here

In-client only:

- the burst landing on the toucher's hand as the toucher and a third observer each see it;
- the real capsule hand's `senderRadius`;
- readout dither at range, which sizes the re-arm band (size it from a two-client observation, not the emulator);
- the coincident-receiver cluster with two wearers together, where the receivers of both copies stack in one spot and an avatar also carrying an owned copy stacks both counts;
- sticky rejection itself, which every pass leans on (`runtime.md` §Contacts);
- whether a slot's first reading after it latches is taken at the front's size or the hold cube's; the dedup rungs tolerate one frame of the former, and more would let a second admission reach the sphere test before dedup and fire as a duplicate.

Dedup and the phantom rungs rest on coincident congruent boxes reading one sender bit-identically in the client, whichever was admitted first and however far the wearer stands from the world origin. The emulator cannot show that; a two-client observation holds it everywhere except the two-wearer cluster above.

## Changing it

- **Regenerate the build as a unit.** Run `python generate.py`, then recompile `built/` as a unit over the committed `.meta`s (`CONVENTIONS.md` §The gate has the procedure). `python generate.py --mesh` regenerates `assets/UnitSphere.obj`.
- **Run the check every time you touch the prefab.** `python generate.py --check` asserts the hand-maintained surface no compile or gate reads: receiver fields and tags, the tilt and its inverse, the node scales and positions dedup rests on, the seam, the World pins, the boundary. Nothing runs it for you and "gates green" never includes it.
- **Changing K.** Set CONFIG's `K`. Duplicate or delete `Slot<k>` subtrees directly under `Cage/Size`, with receiver parameters `CR/Slot<k>/…`, the tilt on the slot and its inverse on `Output`. The geometry must be exact. A slot offset or scaled by more than dedup's tolerance still tracks and still bursts, but it stops recognising the second admissions the front makes every pass and ends up holding hands another slot already holds; that is why `--check` asserts every slot's position, scale and box placement. Regenerate the build; `--check` counts slots against K.
- **Turning on `fourBox`.** Add an `X−` beside each slot's `X+ Y+ Z+`: duplicate the `X+` node, turn it 180° about Y so its +Z face is the cage's −X face, and point its parameter at `CR/Slot<k>/X-`. Then regenerate, since the readout coefficients change. `--check` fails the receiver count and parameter set until every slot has it.
- **Another sender.** Set CONFIG's `tags` and every receiver's collision tags; `--check` compares them.
- **An owned copy** carries the tilt, the slot geometry and the box fields by hand, under the same exactness rule as a new slot, and regenerates its document through `document(overrides)`, which applies its overrides over this CONFIG, so a default changed here reaches the copy at its next regeneration. A key CONFIG does not carry is refused at that door, so a knob this generator drops fails a copy loudly. `check_prefab` in `generate.py` is the door a copy calls to hold the rig.

## Verifying the install

Your own hands are excluded by design, so nothing fires when you test alone. Drive a scripted `HandR` sender (`emulator.md` §Fake another player's contact) or use two clients. Run `--check` first (§Changing it); it catches the geometry, receiver and seam faults of the prefab asset, and nothing else below.

Enable on, walk. `Cage` rides the Hips, world-aligned, at world scale 1 whatever the avatar's scale; finding it at the module's mount point means the BoneProxy never resolved. A `Marker` sits upright while its `Boxes` sit tilted; a tilted marker or upright boxes means the tilt and its inverse have come apart, the failure mode of a hand-copied rig. A menu toggle that does nothing means the enable came out of the build prefixed: `globalParams` must be exactly `[ContactRadar/Enable]`. Two instances on one avatar show as one toggle driving both. With nothing inside, one slot's cube at a time grows from the centre to the face, shrinks to nothing and grows again, indefinitely; a cube resting at full size means no controller drives the slots or it is not this generator's current output. Switch Enable off and on with the sender inside the sphere: every `Boxes` collapses, then the lowest slot's cube grows from the centre and the sender takes its slot and bursts as the front reaches it. With two senders inside, at each handoff the incoming slot's shut cube must be the size the outgoing cube had on its last admitting frame; larger means the front freeze or `CR/SweepPrev` is broken, unless that handoff's `Hit` edge was missed. A burst that fires with nothing visible means the `Burst` to `Emit` sub-emitter link was dropped by a re-edit; it is a non-child reference Unity's inspector will not author, and nothing checks it.

Dedup shows on every pass: with one sender held inside the sphere, each time the front reaches it a second slot admits it and releases itself as soon as its readings land, with no second burst, while the first slot holds it throughout. A second slot that keeps it means the slots' boxes are not coincident (§Changing it). The distance-hide path is the one behaviour only a recipe exercises: set the local runtime's `IsAnimatorEnabled` false, disable its Animator a few frames later, move the senders, then re-enable both. Every `Boxes` reads collapsed from the frame after the flag, and the resume re-acquires the senders now present, each one inside the sphere firing the payload.
