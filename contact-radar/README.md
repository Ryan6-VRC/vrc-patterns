# contact-radar — per-sender contact slots with an exact burst origin (Module)

A particle burst wherever another player's right hand enters a sphere — or a vertical cylinder — around you: several hands at once, each burst on its own hand, no retrigger for a hand dithering at the edge, and everything sensed per client so the burst lands on the hand as each observer sees it. Nothing here syncs but the enable — every copy of the avatar runs its own sensing, which is the only way the burst can sit on the hand where that observer's client draws it. **One synced bit; 3K receivers per copy.**

Two modes, one generator flag, one rig. **Dwell** (the root prefab) holds a hand from cube entry to cube exit and re-bursts each time it comes back inside the burst radius after retreating past the re-arm radius — poke, pull back, poke again — with the payload on the whole time the hand is inside the sphere, so a mesh in it rides the hand. **Entry** (`entry-mode/`) releases the slot a few frames after the burst (the burst state's own dwell, set by its Direct tree's data-dependent length), so a hand has to leave the acquisition cube and come back for another; a hand that stays in fires once. Pick dwell for a reactive bubble, entry for a doorbell. Ships at a 1 m sphere with 0.10 m of hysteresis in both modes; `Cage/Size` rescales the whole thing in the inspector (§Knobs). The zone can be drawn (§Ground truth). Hands already inside when the module comes up are not missed: at enable, at load and after a distance-hide the cube sweeps outward over a second and acquires each one alone as the front reaches it — loud from the toggle (a puff per hand, the shield coming up), silent from a load or a resume (the shield was always running).

**Provenance:** `box-tracker`'s face-proximity readout (VRLabs Contact-Tracker ancestry) with the fourth box removed and the sender radius supplied by config; the buffer-particle form is hfcRed/VRLabs Particle-Bufferer (MIT); the per-sender slot protocol — arm shut, open one at a time, hand off in the same evaluation, re-arm by scale collapse — is this entry's own, resting on the emulator measurements in §How it works.

## Ground truth

- **Seam:** VRCFury `FullController` on the prefab root, FX, `basis: mount-root`; the hierarchy names are the clip paths, so a rename under `Cage` silently unbinds a slot. `HomeAnchor` is an MA `BoneProxy` to Hips, referenced only as a constraint source — no clip path runs through it. `globalParams` is exactly `[ContactRadar/Enable]`; every internal name sits under `CR/` and instance-prefixes at build. Depends on the VRC SDK, VRCFury and Modular Avatar.
- **The menu front is one VRCFury `Toggle` on `ContactRadar/Enable`, synced, unsaved, default on.** Off stows the receivers and zeroes their floats on every client; on starts a loud sweep, so a hand already inside when you switch on gets its own slot and its burst as the front reaches it. **One instance per avatar** — the bare Toggle exports an unprefixed name, and two instances would collide on it.
- **Three ways in, one sweep, two voices.** The toggle (Disabled → Armed) sweeps loud. A fresh animator — a load, a late joiner's copy, a manual hide/show, a mirror clone — starts in `Boot` and sweeps silent: a hand found inside the sphere gets the marker and no puff in dwell mode, and is released without a burst in entry mode. A distance-hide fires `IsAnimatorEnabled` false one frame before it halts the animator (`docs/runtime.md` §Parameters carries the citation); every state parks in `Paused` on that frame, which collapses the boxes, and the resume passes through a collapse step before the silent sweep, so whatever the receivers did during the pause is discarded and the hands present at resume are re-acquired from scratch. Silent ends with the sweep: a hand that crosses in afterwards bursts loud.
- **View cull is the installer's to close, with renderer bounds.** A view-culled copy's animator stops with no signal, so a hand that entered while an observer looked away would burst late when they look back. An observer whose camera is inside any of your renderers' bounds never view-culls you, so set the avatar body's SkinnedMeshRenderer bounds to cover the working volume plus a toucher's reach — a 3 m half-extent around the hips at `Cage/Size` 1, more if you scale the knob up — and every toucher, whose head is an arm from their hand, keeps your animator running whichever way they face. This entry ships no renderer for it: a bounds-only mesh costs a renderer and a material slot on every copy for what one field on the body already does. An observer far away and looking away is the accepted residual; compose `anti-cull` to close that too.
- **Receivers are `localOnly: 0` and `allowSelf: 0` by necessity.** Remote copies must sense so the burst reproduces on every client; your own hand is excluded so a wearer's own gestures never fire it. Tag `HandR` is the config's; retag for another sender.
- **`Cage` is pinned to world rotation and world scale** by two constraints sourcing `assets/World.prefab`, a never-instantiated asset that resolves as world origin on every client, and rides the Hips by position only. World rotation keeps the cube's corners from sweeping a parked hand out and back in when you turn; world scale keeps every radius in absolute metres under avatar scaling. Do not instantiate or delete `World.prefab`, and never Activate either pin — their offsets must stay zero.
- **The boundary is opt-in, and the controller draws it.** `Cage/Size/Boundary` ships inactive holding two unit meshes, `Sphere` and `Cylinder`, each on the built-in default material. The Sweep layer writes both meshes' scale from CONFIG in every state and enables only the configured shape's renderer, only while the toggle is on, so what is drawn is the burst surface by construction — never the re-arm band, never the other shape — and a shape or radius change needs no prefab edit; only the saved renderer enable, which `--check` holds to the configured shape, keeps the edit-mode preview honest. The tube is the lateral wall alone, its ends undrawn. Both meshes are single-sided with outward normals and carry no UVs, so the built-in default material shows nothing from inside — the swap wants a `Cull Off` unlit (`overlay-shaders`). Activate the node and swap the material to use it; an optimizer that strips inactive objects removes it when you do not, which is the intended cost of leaving it off.
- **Distance culling is the one pause the rig handles by signal; view culling is the bounds requirement above.** A client that has distance-culled you cannot see the burst either; what matters is that its copy comes back correct, and the `Paused` path makes it.

## Knobs

One knob is a transform; the rest live in `generate.py`'s CONFIG and the prefab — regenerate, recompile `built/` as a unit, and the README stays as it is.

| Knob | Direction |
|---|---|
| `Cage/Size` (transform scale, on your instance) | the whole sensor at once — cubes, readout, sphere, both bands — since every radius is metres in this node's frame, and it ships at uniform 1. Hold it uniform, between 0.5 and 2.0: the one term that does not scale is the sender-radius bias, so the burst origin lands `senderRadius × (scale − 1)` off the hand per axis, inward when smaller and outward when larger (emulator-measured at 0.5 and 2.0: 2.5 cm and 5 cm per axis, inside the 2 s puff's own spread). Past that range, or for an exact origin at any other size, retune CONFIG and regenerate instead |
| `K` | slots = simultaneous hands before a newcomer is missed until it re-enters the cube; each slot is one FX layer and three receivers on every copy, and the receiver cluster bug (`docs/runtime.md` §Contacts) is reached when two wearers stand together at high K. Other K: duplicate a `Slot<k>` subtree under `Cage` (receiver parameters `CR/Slot<k>/…`), set CONFIG, regenerate |
| `shape` | `sphere` or `cylinder`. A cylinder is vertical about the cage centre — world-vertical, since `Cage` is pinned to world rotation — and in dwell mode its ends take the same hysteresis band as the radius, so there is one band to size against the readout noise; in entry mode, as with the sphere, the re-arm is the acquisition cube face. Drag `HomeAnchor/Offset` to move the column; it does not follow the floor |
| `halfHeight` | cylinder only: half the column's height, about the cage centre; the ends must sit inside the acquisition cube like the sphere must |
| `burstRadius`, `rearmRadius` | the sphere and the dwell band; the band must exceed the client's temporal noise on the readout at the world's working distance (`docs/runtime.md` §Contacts) or a parked hand dithers a burst. The band is the only re-arm dwell mode has; entry mode's is the cube face below |
| `acqHalf`, `holdHalf` | the acquisition cube a hand must enter and the larger hold cube it tracks in. The acquisition face sits `acqHalf − burstRadius − senderRadius` outside the sphere on axis, and that gap is two things at once: the head start the four-frame pipeline gets before a fast hand is inside the sphere (it only decides how far inside a swipe bursts), and in entry mode the re-arm surface — a hand must retract that far past the sphere to burst again, so it is entry's hysteresis. Both ship small, because `acqHalf` is also what admits a hand that was never going to poke you: a bigger acquisition cube fills K faster. `holdHalf − acqHalf` is the admission hysteresis, the drift a held hand gets before it releases |
| `senderRadius` | the constant bias of the hand sender's nearest surface toward each face; measure it on a real capsule hand in-client and retune here, or re-derive it per sender from a fourth `X−` box exactly as `box-tracker` does (`fourBox`, below) |
| `fourBox` | measure the sender's radius from an opposed pair of boxes instead of assuming `senderRadius`, so the burst origin is exact for a hand of any radius and at any `Cage/Size`. It wants a rig the shipped prefab does not carry, and ships off: add an `X−` beside each slot's `X+ Y+ Z+` — duplicate the `X+` node, turn it 180° about Y so its +Z face is the cage's −X face, and point its parameter at `CR/Slot<k>/X-` — and `--check` refuses every slot that lacks one. The measurement is exported per slot as the AAP `CR/Slot<k>/R`, metres in the `Size` frame, for a consumer that needs the radius itself; `senderRadius` then reaches nothing but the reachability lints, which still size the cube against it as the assumed maximum |
| `stepSeconds` | every dwell that must span collision steps; never below two steps |
| `sweepSeconds` | the front's travel time from the cage centre to just past the acquisition face, on every sweep. Two hands whose Chebyshev radii differ by less than about two collision steps of front travel co-latch, so a slower sweep separates closer hands and a faster one looks snappier; the lint refuses a front that crosses more than a quarter of the cube per step |
| `lookupSegments` | the x² table resolution; the chord error is one-sided and inward, so a coarse table shrinks the effective burst radius slightly |
| `enableDefault` | the enable parameter's default in the emitted document; False for a module that should load quiet. The enable is `saved: false`, so every fresh animator takes this default — at False both Boot states fall straight through to `Disabled` without sweeping, and the first sweep is the toggle's own loud one. The VRCFury `Toggle`'s default on the prefab is the same bit from the other side and must agree; the shipped prefab has it on |

## How it works

Receivers aggregate senders, strongest wins, so per-sender behaviour exists only through the latch: the allow flags are checked when an overlap begins and never again. Three facts measured in av3emu carry the whole protocol. **Rejection is sticky** — a sender whose overlap began while `allowOthers` was shut is absent from that receiver's collision set for as long as the overlap lasts, whatever the flag does later, on Proximity, Constant and OnEnter alike. **A scale collapse and restore is a fresh episode** for every sender present, with no deaf failure: too short for a step is a silent no-op, never a false zero (a GameObject bounce that short comes back deaf). **A same-evaluation flag handoff between two receivers partitions senders exactly**, and a sender acquired in the one step before the flip is double-admitted.

Each slot is one layer (`generate.py`'s docstring walks the states). What is not derivable from the document: the ring rule opens the next Armed slot in the same evaluation the Open slot latches, so the two land in one collision step and the admission windows tile; when nothing is Open, the lowest Armed slot opens itself on its clip's exit-time crossing, quantized so the flags it reads are fresh; a hand read by only some of a slot's three coincident boxes (it happens on the frame the slot opens) parks the slot in Partial for a step and then Recycles it, because an Open slot that stalls blocks every admission on the avatar. The burst is arithmetic, not a receiver: the tree sums `x = 2h·X+ − h − r` per axis into the AAPs and `Output`'s localPosition, three 1D lookups add x², y², z² into r², and the transition compares r² against the two radii — which is what makes the zone a sphere and the hysteresis per-sender. A cylinder is the same tree with y² left out of the sum and y compared against the half-height directly, so r² is the in-plane radius and the ends are a second predicate on the same readout; the axis compare reads y one AAP hop fresher than r² reads x and z, a one-frame disagreement at a corner, inside the four-frame trail below.

The front is the same protocol run outward from the centre. While the shared `CR/Sweeping` flag is up every Armed slot sits collapsed, so it holds no rejections, and the one slot in `Sweep` rides its cube on the shared `CR/Sweep` front with the flag up: a hand's overlap begins when the growing face reaches its surface, so each is admitted alone. On the latch the ring opens the next slot into `SweepShut` — its cube appears at the current front shut for a step, re-rejecting the hand just latched and anything else inside the front (the Recycle idiom) — and the front only advances while a flag-up cube is riding it, so the shut step never sweeps a hand it would reject. The sweep ends when the front passes the acquisition face: that slot goes `Open` at full size, the `Sweep` layer goes `Idle`, and the Armed slots restore shut with nothing unlatched left inside. The four shared AAPs (`Sweeping`, `Silent`, `Sweep`, `SweepBase`) have exactly one writer, the `Sweep` layer, whose every state writes all four — a WD-ON blend-tree state does not hold an AAP it leaves unwritten (measured: it reverts to the default on the next frame), so a value that must persist is written back to itself through a direct child weighted by its own value. Slot layers read the four and report their own `Front` flag for the layer to ramp on. `Silent` picks the endpoint for a hand the front finds inside the sphere: `TrackInSilent` in dwell mode holds `Payload` on with the buffer particle's own GameObject off, so the marker shows and nothing is born; entry mode releases straight to `Recycle`.

The burst states toggle one node, `Payload`, on while the hand is inside the sphere, and it serves two consumers at once because they read it differently: `Burst` inside it is a renderer-less buffer particle born at `Output` on the frame the wrapper enables (the edge — one puff per entry, however long the hand stays), and a mesh inside it is visible for as long as the wrapper is on (the level — the dwell demo's `Marker` rides the hand until it retreats past the re-arm radius). `Emit`, the visible burst, is `Burst`'s Birth sub-emitter and sits outside the wrapper, directly under `Output`, never disabled — disabling it is what truncates a burst in flight — and its sub-emitter reference is a non-child link Unity's inspector will not author; if a re-edit drops it, the buffer fires and nothing visible emits.

## Traps

- **The co-latch window.** A Proximity reading costs two collision steps, so a second hand whose overlap began within about two steps of the first is admitted by the same slot and rejected by the next for its whole overlap. The readout is then a per-axis *maximum* over the two hands — a corner that is never nearer the centre than either hand on same-sign approaches (it delays or suppresses a burst) and can sit inside the sphere when neither hand does only when the hands approach on opposite signs of different axes: one burst at a phantom point, and the second hand invisible until it exits the cube and re-enters. Two right hands crossing the cube face within about 1/30 s. The unbuilt refinement is one shared OnEnter receiver over the cube, flag always open, whose pulse triggers the Open slot a step earlier.
- **Two hands at one Chebyshev radius co-latch on a sweep.** The front reaches both within the two collision steps a Proximity reading costs, so the sweeping slot admits both and reads their per-axis maximum (measured: hands at (0.5, 0, 0) and (0, 0.5, 0) read `1, 1, 0.55` in one slot). `sweepSeconds` is the resolution; the co-latch window above is the same mechanism.
- **Exhaustion mid-sweep leaves `Sweeping` up.** With every slot latched nothing sweeps and nothing clears the flag; the next recycled slot resumes the front from where it stopped and finishes the sweep — silently, if the sweep was — so a hand that arrives during that gap and is found by the resumed front may take the silent endpoint. K hands present at enable is the case.
- **A view-culled observer still misses what happened while they looked away**, and inflated body bounds only guarantee that an observer *inside* them never view-culls you. A far observer who turns back sees the state the pause left, bursts included; that is the declared residual, and `anti-cull` is the remedy.
- **The boundary can hide a hand that still bursts.** The drawn surface is the burst surface; the re-arm band lies outside it, so a hand that has retreated a little past the mesh still holds the payload until it clears the band.
- **The burst trails the hand by about four frames of travel.** The readout lands one frame behind the hand and the r² predicate three more (two collision steps for a Proximity value, one AAP hop, one transition read), so the burst fires inside the sphere by the hand's speed times four frames — a few centimetres for a slow poke, tens of centimetres for a swipe — and `Output` on the burst frame sits one frame of travel behind the hand. Widen `burstRadius` if fast hands matter.
- **A hand that stays inside holds its slot** (dwell): a friend standing with a hand in your cube occupies one of K until they move it out of the hold cube. Entry mode releases at the burst instead.
- **Exhaustion.** The Open slot fires with no Armed slot left: nothing opens, and a hand entering meanwhile is rejected by every slot until it leaves the cube and re-enters. In dwell mode that is K hands inside the cube; in entry mode it is K hands loitering in the shell between the sphere and the cube face — with a cylinder that shell also covers everything past the ends inside the cube, most of its volume at a small `halfHeight`. No preemption ships: evicting a tracked hand to serve a newcomer would cost a victim ladder per slot and still miss the hand it evicted.
- **Every step-spanning dwell is authored in seconds, never frames**, and every slot state writes every flag, the payload toggle and the buffer toggle, zeros included — a state that wrote only its own 1 would leave `Open` stuck and the ring rule firing forever. The generator enforces both; a hand edit of `controller.yaml` is how they break.
- **The readout coefficients and the hold cube are one unit.** `holdHalf` is baked into the readout clips, and `senderRadius` with it unless `fourBox` measures it; resize the boxes only through CONFIG.
- **3K coincident receivers on every copy.** Two wearers side by side approach the ~24-receiver cluster bug as K grows; K is the knob and off stows them.
- **Nothing here is faithful in-client until measured there:** the emulator's readings are bit-constant, the client's dither with distance from the world origin, and the client refreshes receivers in only a fraction of frames. The dwell band and the entry-mode cube margin are the remedies; size them from a two-client observation, not from the emulator.

Deliberately not shipped: the shared OnEnter refinement above, an `allowSelf` self-test variant (it changes what the pattern senses), one `Emit` shared across slots (a possible material-count optimization, untested), and preemption.

## Entry mode

`entry-mode/ContactRadarEntry.prefab` is a prefab **variant** of `ContactRadar.prefab` at its own CONFIG's lower K. It customises exactly three things, each stated here because a removal is unvalidated (`docs/nondestructive.md`): the inherited root `FullController` is removed and re-added pointing at `entry-mode/built/`; the slots above its K are removed as variant overrides; and each kept slot's `Marker` is removed, because here `Payload` is on only for the burst state's few frames and a mesh inside it would flash (keep one if a flash on poke is what you want). A silent sweep here releases a hand found inside the sphere without a burst — it then has to leave the cube to burst, entry's ordinary re-arm — and tracks a hand found in the shell, which bursts loud when it crosses in. `generate.py --check` names every removal. Every other node is inherited, so a retune of the shared rig reaches it without a diff. Its `built/` carries its own GUIDs.

## Verifying the install

Enable on, walk: `Cage` rides the Hips, world-aligned, at world scale 1 whatever the avatar's scale — finding it at the avatar root means the BoneProxy never resolved. Put a scripted `HandR` sender (`docs/emulator.md` §Fake another player's contact) straight in along one cage axis: the first slot's three floats leave zero together, its flag shuts and its boxes expand on one frame while the next slot's flag opens on the same frame, `Output` sits on the sender, and `Payload` enables as the sender crosses the burst radius with `Emit` emitting. Pull the sender past the re-arm radius and back: a second burst (dwell) or none until it leaves the cube (entry). In dwell the `Marker` cube rides the sender while it is inside the sphere and vanishes as it retreats past the re-arm radius; the slot itself is held until the sender leaves the hold cube. Scale `Cage/Size` on the instance and every distance above scales with it. Activate `Cage/Size/Boundary` and enter play: the configured shape's renderer comes on with the toggle and reads its world size as twice the burst radius across, and twice the half-height tall for a cylinder; the other shape's renderer stays off. With `shape: cylinder`, a sender inside the burst radius in-plane but above the half-height reads its y on `Output` and never gets the payload, one lifted past the re-arm height from inside drops it and holds it dropped inside the band, and one lowered back inside gets it again. A second sender arriving while the first is inside gets its own slot and its own burst. Park three senders inside at different radii, switch Enable off and on: every `Boxes` collapses, the lowest slot's cube grows from the centre at about `acqHalf / sweepSeconds` (the ramp aims 5 % past the face so the exit fires before the clip ends), each sender is admitted as the front reaches its surface (nearest first), gets its own slot and — inside the sphere — its own puff, and the slot after the last one grows to the face and opens at full size while the remaining Armed slots restore. Spawn a non-local clone with the senders still inside: its slots sweep the same way but every `Payload` comes on with `Burst`'s GameObject off (marker, no puff). Set the local runtime's `IsAnimatorEnabled` false, disable its Animator a few frames later, move the senders, re-enable both: every `Boxes` read collapsed from the frame after the flag, and the resume re-acquires the senders now present, silently.

What stays in-client only: the burst landing on the toucher's hand as the toucher and a third observer each see it, the real capsule hand's `senderRadius`, threshold dither at range, and the cluster bug with two wearers together.
