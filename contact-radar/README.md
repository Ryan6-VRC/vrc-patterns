# contact-radar (Module)

A per-hand tracker: latches onto each other player's hand that enters a vertical cylinder around you, one slot per hand, and exposes where that hand is so you can attach anything to it. Sensing runs on every client, so an attached prop sits on the hand where that client draws it. The shipped payload is a particle burst on entry; the production consumers of this entry attach props to the tracked hand.

## How it works

- Box contacts are selectively enabled and latched to track a series of matching senders as they enter and leave the zone. Each tracked hand occupies one of K slots (K is how many hands can be tracked at once). A slot is a set of coincident box contacts whose size, position and `allowOthers` flag the animator controls. With the flag up a box takes a hand it newly touches; with it shut, the box ignores that hand until their overlap ends.
- The **front** is the face of a cube that grows from the centre to full size (the **acquisition cube**) over `sweepSeconds`, collapses, and grows again; each growth is a **pass**. One free slot (one holding no hand) at a time **rides** the front: its own boxes are that growing cube, flag up. The riding cube takes a hand as the front reaches it, while the other free slots wait collapsed, so no flag-up cube ever appears around hands already inside it. A hand that arrives is taken on the next pass to reach it.
- The riding slot learns that *it* took a hand from its own boxes: one collision step (the contact system's update tick, once per frame at 60 fps and below) after its cube took the hand, every box in it reads above zero. On that frame it works out where the hand is from those readings, shuts its flags, and shrinks its boxes to a small cluster (the **placement cluster**) at that point. The next free slot takes over the front at the size the front had when the hand was taken.
- Two collision steps later the slot grows its cluster to the **hold cluster**, still small, and from then on moves it with the hand every frame. The animator decodes the hand's offset from the cluster's centre, writes the hand's position, and moves the centre part of the way toward it. A held hand therefore costs one contact pair per receiver per sender inside its cluster, not per sender anywhere near you.
- The readout feeds a blend tree that sums the squares into r², takes the height's square back out for the in-plane radius², and an animator transition compares that against two radii, so the zone is a vertical cylinder with per-hand hysteresis. Nothing bounds its height. A hand is let go when it passes the **bound** (the acquisition cube grown by the hold cluster) or the cluster loses it.
- The cube is tilted so its body diagonal is vertical. The growing front then reaches a standing player's senders (head, hips, hands, where the tags match several) one at a time instead of all in one collision step, so each usually takes its own slot (§Traps). The readout lives in the tilted frame; the tree undoes the tilt on the vertical axis alone, as the hand's height above the cage centre, which is all the cylinder needs.
- In a crowd the contact system can drop a held hand for one collision step. The slot then **reacquires**: it collapses its boxes for a step, reopens a placement cluster flag-up where the hand last was, and takes the hand back with the payload level unbroken. If the hand is not there, the slot is given back.
- Every pass, the front also reaches each held hand still inside the acquisition cube, and the riding slot takes it too. A dedup layer compares the slots' last decoded positions and releases the second slot on one hand. It also releases a slot whose reading merges several hands other slots hold.
- Nothing is synced but the enable.

## Install guide

1. Know what a slot does. A hand keeps its slot as long as its cluster keeps up with it and it stays inside the bound, and no slot with a stronger claim reads the same hand (§Design notes, dedup), so a prop hung on the slot follows the hand for as long as it is there; the shipped burst re-fires each time the hand pulls back a short distance and comes in again. For a doorbell, read the payload's rising edge and ignore its level (§Design notes, what a consumer reads).
2. Drop an instance of `ContactRadar.prefab` at the avatar root. `HomeAnchor` snaps to the Hips on its own; drag its `Offset` child to move where the zone sits. Edits belong on the instance or an owned copy, never on the package asset.
3. Set the body SkinnedMeshRenderer's Bounds (its `localBounds`) to reach √3 · (`acqHalf + followHalf`) plus an arm's length in every direction from the hips: a cluster can follow a hand that far toward the tilted cube's corners, and the camera that must sit inside the bounds is the toucher's head, an arm beyond the hand. VRChat pauses a copy's animator while its observer is not looking at you, and this rig gets no signal for that, so a hand that entered while an observer looked away would burst late. An observer whose camera is inside any of your renderers' bounds never pauses you; with the bounds set, every toucher keeps your animator running. A far observer looking away is the accepted residual; compose `anti-cull` to close that too.
4. Scale `Cage/Size` if the zone should be larger or smaller. Uniform scale only, shipped at 1; it scales the zone, the band, the clusters, the readout and the hysteresis together.
5. Build. The expression menu gains a `Contact Radar` toggle, on by default.

Depends on the VRC SDK, VRCFury and Modular Avatar.

## How to use

- `ContactRadar/Enable` is the one parameter: synced, unsaved, on by default. Off stows the receivers and zeroes them on every client.
- Hang your prop under a slot's `Output`; it is the reconstructed hand position, in metres, and stays world-aligned. It trails the hand by the few frames the follower's pairing takes (§Knobs, `pairDelay`). Every slot needs its own copy. `Output` is always active, so a prop under it never hides on its own. A free slot's `Output` sits at the cage centre, and once per pass the riding slot's `Output` jumps onto a hand another slot already holds for the frames until dedup releases it. While a slot reacquires, its `Output` holds where the hand last was. A prop that must show only while a hand is inside the zone belongs under `Payload`.
- A controller merged into this prefab's FullController (`gimmicks.md` §Packaging and interface) reads the same position from `CR/Slot<k>/x`, `y`, `z` (the tilted frame), the hand's height above the cage centre from `yw`, its squared distance from the centre from `r2` and its squared horizontal distance, the value the zone compares, from `p2`. While a slot is latching, `x` sits at `+(acqHalf + followHalf)` and `r2` and `p2` read outside every zone; while it reacquires, `r2` and `p2` stop tracking (0 at Write Defaults on).
- `Payload` under `Output` is the node the controller turns on while the hand is inside the zone: put a mesh there and it shows while the hand is inside; a buffer particle there fires once on entry. `Marker` and `Burst` are the shipped examples of each.
- Replace `Marker` (a placeholder cube on the default material) with your mesh, or delete it.
- Restyle `Emit`, the visible burst, which ships on Unity's default particle sprite, or delete `Emit` and `Burst` together if you want no burst. Never disable `Emit` while keeping it; disabling it truncates a burst in flight.
- Activate `Boundary` to draw the zone. Its `Cylinder` is an open tube on the default material, which is invisible from inside, so give it a `Cull Off` unlit (`overlay-shaders`). The controller scales it to the burst radius and to the band inside which that radius is reached from every direction (§Knobs), and shows it only while the toggle is on; the zone reaches past the tube's ends, direction-dependently.
- To sense another sender than `HandR`, see §Changing it.

## Additional notes

- **One instance per avatar.** The menu control is a bare VRCFury `Toggle`, which exports an unprefixed name; two instances would collide on it.
- **Your own hands never fire it.** The wearer is excluded by design, so nothing happens when you test alone (§Verifying the install).
- **Do not rename anything under `Cage`.** The clip paths are the hierarchy names; a rename silently unbinds a slot.
- **Leave `assets/World.prefab` alone.** It is a never-instantiated asset that resolves as the world origin on every client, and two constraints on `Cage` source it. Do not instantiate it, delete it, or press Activate on those constraints: their offsets must stay zero.
- **The receivers count against your rank.** They cannot be local-only, since remote copies must sense for the tracking to reproduce, so all 3K of them count toward the SDK's contact-count statistic (`optimization.md`). At the shipped K they sit inside the Good allowance with a few contacts to spare; `fourBox` fills it on its own, so `fourBox` plus any other non-local contact on the avatar crosses to Medium, and the 2K particle systems cap the avatar at Medium independently; raising K pushes both.
- **`Cage/Size` far from 1 shifts the readout.** Everything in the rig is metres in that node's frame except the sender-radius terms: the latch's placement offset, and under three boxes the readout's bias. So `Output` lands up to `senderRadius × (scale − 1)` off the hand per axis; beyond a modest scale, turn on `fourBox` to remove the readout's term or retune `senderRadius` (§Knobs).
- **An optimizer that strips inactive objects** removes `Boundary` when you leave it off, which is intended. `Payload` is also inactive at rest with `Marker` and `Burst` inside it; an optimizer that strips inactive subtrees takes both.

## Knobs

`Cage/Size` is the transform above. Everything else is `generate.py`'s CONFIG: edit, regenerate, recompile `built/` as a unit (§Changing it). Each row gives the direction and the relation; the value lives in the code (the shipped zone radius is `burstRadius` there), and a lint in `generate.py` refuses a value that breaks the relation.

Terms used below, beside the front, pass, acquisition cube, placement and hold clusters and bound of §How it works. The acquisition cube is the front's full size: the front runs a little past its face, then collapses to start the next pass. The **bound** is `acqHalf + followHalf` on each tilted axis. The **re-arm band** is the ring between the burst radius and the re-arm radius. The **approach gap** is the space between the cylinder and the acquisition cube's face. The **band** is the range of heights about the cage centre inside which a hand at the burst radius is inside the acquisition cube from every direction; the tilted cube's horizontal cross-section is widest at the centre height and shrinks toward its top and bottom corners, so above and below the band the cube still reaches, but less far in some directions than others. A hand's **tilted radius** is its distance from the centre as the tilted cube measures it, the largest of its three distances along the cube's axes. A **phantom** is one slot reading two or more hands at once, their per-axis maximum.

|---|---|
| `K` | Hands tracked at once. Each slot costs one FX layer and three receivers on every copy (four with `fourBox`); the dedup parameters grow with K squared, and each slot's phantom rungs number (K − 1)³ − (K − 1) in each of its three tracking states. Past a couple of dozen coincident receivers, measure the configuration in-client rather than trust either number (`runtime.md` §Contacts). Lint: at least two, since one slot has nothing to hand off to. Changing K also means changing the prefab (§Changing it) |
| `burstRadius`, `rearmRadius` | The cylinder's radius, and the outer edge of the re-arm band. The re-arm band must exceed the client's readout noise at the world's working distance (`runtime.md` §Contacts) or a parked hand dithers a burst. Lint: the re-arm radius above the burst radius, and reachable from every direction at the centre height, which is where the band's half-height, `band_half_height` in the code, is above zero |
| `acqHalf` | The acquisition cube's half-size. The approach gap depends on direction and height: widest sideways at the centre height, shrinking to nothing at the band's edge, and absent along a cube axis; straight up or down there is no zone boundary at all. Where there is a gap, a hand the front reaches in it is held before it enters the zone and fires on crossing it; the overlap begins when the hand's surface reaches the front, so the gap is a sender radius wider than the geometry says. `acqHalf` also sets the band: its half-height grows by root three per metre of `acqHalf` and shrinks by root two per metre of burst radius. It ships small, because a bigger cube fills K with hands that were never going to poke you, and a bigger `acqHalf` at the same `sweepSeconds` moves the front faster. Lints: the band above zero at the re-arm radius; the box within the SDK's serialized limit; the bound (`acqHalf + followHalf`) within the registers' range (`reg_max` in the code) |
| `followHalf` | The hold cluster's half-size. Larger keeps a faster hand inside the cluster at a slow client's frame rate, and admits more of the crowd's senders into the cluster's pairs; smaller does the reverse. It also extends the bound, and with it the bounds an install needs (Install guide step 3). Lints: above `placeHalf`, below `acqHalf`; the bound within the registers' range |
| `placeHalf` | The placement cluster's half-size, where every latch and every reacquire opens. Smaller drops more same-step merges (§Design notes, the placement) and admits fewer neighbours when a reacquire opens flag-up; larger forgives a worse decode. Lints: above `senderRadius`, so a hand's surface at the decoded point is inside it; `placeHalf + senderRadius` above √3 · `senderRadius`, so a hand of up to twice the assumed radius still lands inside; and `placeHalf` above two steps of front travel (`2 · acqHalf · stepSeconds / sweepSeconds`), the latch's residual on the admitting axis, which is up to two steps because the decode's front size is one step past the admitting cube's |
| `followGain` | The fraction of the decoded offset the cluster's centre moves each frame. Higher keeps closer to a fast hand; below one keeps the follower stable when the pairing is a frame off (§Design notes). Lint: above zero, at most one |
| `pairDelay` | How many animator evaluations pass between a write of the cluster's centre and the first reading taken against it, which selects the copy of the centre the follower adds its offset to. A value off by one makes the follower carry a frame of the hand's travel as error, bounded at a gain below one. Lint: 1, 2 or 3 |
| `senderRadius` | The senders' typical radius: the offset the latch subtracts to place the cluster at the hand's centre rather than its surface, and under three boxes the readout's constant bias. Set it to your senders' typical radius, not a maximum. Under `fourBox` the readout measures the radius instead, but the latch still uses this one |
| `fourBox` | Measure the sender radius from an opposed pair of boxes instead of assuming it, so the readout is exact for any hand at any `Cage/Size`. Costs one receiver per slot. Exported per slot as `CR/Slot<k>/R`; a reading of `R` past `senderRadius + dedupBand` means the two X boxes are reading two different hands, and the slot is released. Needs an `X−` box the shipped prefab does not carry (§Changing it) |
| `stepSeconds` | Every timed wait that must span collision steps: the front's restart at the face, the recycle collapse, the reacquire's collapse, and twice it the placement's wait. A wait inside a tree state (the placement's, the grow, the three reacquire states, the fresh window) is its configured base plus the tree's register term, never exact; `controller.yaml` states the computed bound beside each, the placement's wait about 0.1 s at the default. Lint: above the longest gap between two steps (`runtime.md` §Contacts) |
| `latchSeconds` | The base of the wait a grown cluster stands before the follower reads it, after a latch and after a reacquire, so a collision step has sampled the grown cluster first; the tree's register term adds to it (`stepSeconds` row). It ships at two `stepSeconds`, not one, because a remote copy's receiver values reach its animator about a frame later than the wearer's own (§Traps). Lint: at least two `stepSeconds` |
| `reacquireSeconds` | The base of how long the placement cluster stands flag-up after a cut before the slot is given back; the tree's register term adds to it (`stepSeconds` row). Longer catches a hand that has drifted further; the reacquiring slot holds the payload and every fresh slot's entry to the zone for that time (§Design notes, the reacquire). Lint: at least two `stepSeconds`, since a returning hand reads only after two collision steps |
| `dedupBand` | How close, in metres per axis, two slots' last decoded positions must be for dedup to call them one hand. Too small and a duplicate decoded with the follower's lag survives; too large and two real hands collapse onto one slot. Lint: above zero |
| `farRadius` | Off (None) by default. When set, a settled hand whose in-plane radius reads past it is released, so a head standing between the zone and the bound stops holding a slot: its slot collapses and forms no contact pairs, and the front re-offers the hand every pass, a short cycle like a dedup re-admission. A settled merge whose point reads past it is released the same way, every pass. The rung runs only in the settled band state (`TrackBand`), never on a fresh admission. Lints: above `rearmRadius`; below the bound |
| `sweepSeconds` | The front's travel time from the centre to the acquisition face. It sets both the period (how often the front offers itself to each hand) and the resolution (how close in tilted radius two hands can be before they share a slot). Slower: a newcomer waits longer and a quick tap is likelier to be missed, but two hands must be closer to share a slot. Faster: the reverse. The sharing window is the front's travel over two collision steps, so a low frame rate widens it. Each held hand the front reaches pauses it for at least `stepSeconds` (a **stall**); `sweepSeconds` plus those stalls is the effective period. Lint: the front may cross no more than a quarter of `acqHalf` in one collision step at 60 fps |
| `lookupSegments` | The square tables' resolution, as segments per fixed reference span (`TABLE_REF` in the code), so the chord width holds as the bound moves; the tables span the bound on each side. Lint: at least four. The axis tables' chord error is one-sided and inward; the height table's, subtracted, is outward, so a coarse table moves the effective burst radius by a little either way |
| `enableDefault` | The enable's default in the document; False loads with the rig off, so the first pass starts when the toggle turns on. The prefab `Toggle`'s default is the same bit from the other side and must agree; `--check` holds the pair |

## Design notes

The state-by-state walk is `generate.py`'s module docstring, at the place an edit would break it, and `controller.yaml` comments every state, the rungs whose reason is not in their conditions, and each dwell's computed length bound. This section states what the rig guarantees and why.

**What a consumer reads.** The readout writes `Output`'s localPosition and `CR/Slot<k>/x`, `y`, `z` in metres in the `Cage/Size` frame (tilted), `yw` in metres, the height above the cage centre, and `r2` and `p2` in metres squared, the distance from the centre and its horizontal part; under `fourBox`, `CR/Slot<k>/R` is the measured sender radius. A latching slot parks `x` at `+(acqHalf + followHalf)` until its follower runs, which a consumer can read as "not yet tracking". During a reacquire the position holds at the last decoded point while `r2` and `p2` stop tracking. `Payload` toggles on while the hand is inside the zone and serves two consumers at once: a buffer particle inside it reads the enable edge (one puff per entry, however long the hand stays) and a mesh inside it reads the level. A second admission that dedup releases never turns `Payload` on, but for its few frames it raises `CR/Slot<k>/Held` and moves the readout like any fresh track. A consumer reading per slot sees those, and sees a hand change slots when a fresh lower-index slot displaces a fresh higher-index one.

The protocol rests on contact-runtime facts in `runtime.md` §Contacts: a receiver aggregates its senders and the strongest wins; rejection at acquisition is sticky for the whole overlap; a host-scale collapse and restore is a fresh overlap episode with no deaf mode; a fresh Proximity receiver reads zero on its first collision step. Two more are this rig's own premises, housed nowhere else in the workspace: a held overlap survives its receiver's scale and position changing so long as it never breaks, which is what lets a cluster shrink onto and follow a hand; and a dense crowd can block a small receiver from pairing where a large one is not blocked (§Traps). Animator timing (`runtime.md` §Animator evaluation) supplies the rest: every parameter read as a blend weight is one frame late, and a tree state keeps a value only by writing it back to itself.

**Sticky rejection is why acquisition is a sweep.** A full-size cube with its allow flag shut rejects every hand already inside for the whole overlap, and a full-size cube with its flag up takes everything that appears inside it on one collision step as one reading. So free slots wait collapsed and shut, holding no rejections, while one cube grows flag-up to the acquisition face. There the front parks at zero for `stepSeconds`, long enough for a collision step to sample it at any frame rate; that collapses the riding cube, ending every overlap it was rejecting, and the next pass grows from the centre with the flag still up. `CR/Slot<k>/Open` reads 1 on the one slot holding the front position (shut at it, riding it, or in the one-step grace a partial admission gets).

**The front changes slots in one evaluation.** The riding slot latches on its own readings, and in the same evaluation the next free slot takes the front and the Sweep layer freezes it, so the successor appears shut at exactly the size that admitted and no radius goes unoffered. One race escapes the freeze: a hand taken on the very frame the front crosses the face has no reading yet, so the restart can win, and the new pass re-offers that hand once while its holder is still fresh. The latch reads the readings rather than a Constant receiver's edge a step earlier as a trade: a slot without that receiver carries one fewer, and in a crowd the rig's own contact-pair load costs more than the narrower merge window the edge bought.

**The placement starts small.** Starting small is what drops most same-step merges: two hands the front reaches on one collision step latch to their per-axis maximum, a corner on neither, and a small cluster there keeps a hand only if it lies within `placeHalf` plus its radius of that corner on every axis, so the slot recycles at once and the next pass offers the hands separately. The latch decodes on one evaluation only, because a tree state re-samples every frame and would decode the shrunk cluster's readings with the front's coefficients. The admitting face reads its clamp, 1.0, because the hand straddles it, so the decode adds two sender radii on any axis reading exactly 1.0: the overlap began at the hand's near surface, and its centre sits that much further out. Without it the cluster opens short of the hand on that axis, toward the cage centre and its neighbours. Under `fourBox` the fourth box rides collapsed, so it never skews the first reading, and the two-step wait after the placement exists because a freshly opened `X-` reads zero on its first colliding step; its second supplies the value `LatchGrow` is entered on.

**The follower.** A reading arrives a few evaluations after the cluster move it was taken against, so the follower adds the decoded offset to the copy of the centre written `pairDelay` evaluations earlier; a wrong `pairDelay` puts a frame of the hand's travel into the position as error, which a gain below one keeps bounded and a gain of one does not. The centre and its copies are stored offset and scaled because a blend weight clamps negative values to zero and a tree state's length grows with its weights. A follower has no face to release at, so the bound rung lets go of a hand that passes `acqHalf + followHalf` on any tilted axis, and the all-1.0 row of a hidden or reloaded sender (`runtime.md` §Contacts) releases before the follower can decode it into a jump.

**The reacquire rides out a cut.** A crowd cut drops every reading of a hand to zero on one collision step while the hand is usually still there. The reacquire collapses first because the returning overlap would otherwise meet the shut flag and be rejected for its whole length. It collapses at the cage centre, as Recycle does, because a collapsed box at the hand's own point is still inside the hand and the rejected overlap would never end; it reopens where the hand last decoded rather than at the lagging centre. One reacquire per cut, so it is bounded by its three dwells. While any slot reacquires, every fresh slot is held back from the zone: the reacquiring slot's position is frozen at the cut, and a rider that re-took the same hand could decode it past `dedupBand` from there; held back, it keeps comparing until the original slot tracks again and dedup releases it without a second burst. The gate guards a hand that moves more than `dedupBand` during a reacquire; for a still hand the duplicate rung releases the rider first. A fresh slot that loses its hand is simply given back.

**A released slot goes straight back onto the front when every lower-index slot is tracking a hand.** Recycle collapses its boxes at the cage centre for one step, which ends every overlap it held; the slot then takes the front directly if nothing else is Armed or holding it, and otherwise waits Armed. The lower-index condition is the tie-break: flags are read a frame late, and two slots released on one frame would otherwise both take the front.

**The cage is pinned to the world.** `Cage` is held at world rotation and world scale by the two `World.prefab` constraints and rides the Hips by position only. World rotation keeps the cube's corners from sweeping a parked hand out and back in when you turn; world scale keeps every radius in absolute metres under avatar scaling. The rotation is the `TILT` constant in `generate.py`; `--check` holds it here and `check_prefab` holds it on an owned copy.

**A distance-hide discards the pause.** A distance-hide announces itself one frame early (`runtime.md` §Parameters and network sync); every slot parks and collapses its boxes on that frame, so whatever the receivers did during the pause is discarded and the hands present at resume are re-acquired from scratch. The Sweep layer is the sole writer of the front's parameters and also draws the boundary, which is why a radius change needs no prefab edit.

**Dedup releases the second slot on a hand.** Every pass re-admits each held hand the front reaches, so dedup is the common path, not a corner. It compares positions rather than readings because two clusters at different places read one hand differently. A slot that has settled (inside the zone, in the re-arm band after it, or held outside the zone past a few frames) wins outright whatever its index; between two fresh slots the lower index wins, and the rungs are asymmetric so neither can release the other. A settled slot keeps comparing against lower-index settled slots, so a merge that has resolved onto a hand another slot holds gives its slot back. A reacquiring slot keeps its last position for dedup, so a rider that re-takes its hand still matches it.

**The phantom rungs release a phantom of held hands.** Two hands closer than the placement cluster's reach on every axis, cheek to cheek in a huddle, both survive the latch, and the slot follows their per-axis maximum, a point on neither. Because a merge is its members' maximum, a rung that matches each axis to a different holder and requires the merge strictly above the other holders there releases it, while leaving alone a real single hand two merges each contain. Under `fourBox` a merge across the two X boxes inflates `R`, which is why `R` past `senderRadius + dedupBand` releases the slot; one separated by less in x has its midpoint within the band of both sources.

## Traps

- **A same-step merge with an unheld hand in it keeps its slot until that hand leaves the cluster.** Two hands the front reaches on one collision step share the riding slot; the placement drops the merge when they are farther apart than the placement cluster's reach on some axis, and otherwise the slot follows their per-axis maximum in the tilted frame. The phantom rungs cannot release it while a member is held nowhere else. A teleport or respawn into a group, or several avatars loading beside you at once, is the common case. The point mostly sits above both hands, so it can read inside the zone when neither is, and fire one burst at a point on no hand. Later passes offer each hand again, and once every member holds a slot of its own the phantom rungs release the merge.
- **In a dense crowd the contact system can fail to pair a small receiver with a hand at all, while a large receiver over the same hand still pairs.** A cage-sized cube is never blocked this way; a cluster at a hand can be, when enough other contact shapes crowd that hand's position. The cost is one hand dropping and returning each pass: the cluster loses it, the slot times out, and the front re-finds it. No rig-side change removes it, and whether a real crowd reaches that density is not known. A flag-up `allowSelf` receiver of placement size on your own hand, logged beside the rig, reads zero only when the crowd blocks a cluster there.
- **A hand faster than the cluster is lost and leaves a short ghost.** A hand that outruns the hold cluster at the client's frame rate reads as a cut: the slot reacquires at the point it last decoded, holds the payload on there for the reacquire wait, then releases, and the front re-finds the hand. Raise `followHalf` or `followGain` if hands are lost in motion.
- **Senders near a held sender cost pairs.** Every sender within `followHalf` plus its radius of a held sender's cluster, on every tilted axis, costs one contact pair per box, so a dense pack of senders around a held head (a hand on it, fingers included) is that cluster's load. When that load is what fills the region's pair budget, the cut recurs on the reacquire's regrow and the slot is given back rather than looping; the front re-finds the sender on its next pass.
- **Two still senders close together are never held while they stand still.** Two senders closer than about twice `placeHalf` plus a radius on every axis, reached by the front on one step, both stay inside the placement cluster, and the `R` bound or the merge releases the slot; every pass merges them again. A sway larger than two steps of front travel on the tilted radius separates them.
- **Under `fourBox`, a small fast sender can escape the placement.** A sender smaller than `senderRadius` arriving faster than about 1.5 m/s can leave the placement cluster during its two-step wait, since the cluster does not follow until tracking starts. It is taken once it slows; `placeHalf` is the knob.
- **Under `farRadius`, an inbound sender can be released before it reaches the zone.** A sender first latched in-plane outside the far radius while moving inward can settle into the band before it crosses in, be released by the far rung, and be re-taken by a later pass. The fresh window that decides when it settles is the follower tree's length.
- **A teleport of the wearer reacquires every settled slot at stale positions.** Every settled slot reopens where its hand used to be relative to you; a stranger standing there is adopted with the payload level unbroken, and otherwise every slot times out together and the front re-finds everyone, one burst per hand inside the zone.
- **A hand standing within one step of front travel inside the acquisition face is never taken until it moves.** The front meets it on the collision step it crosses the face, the readings that would latch it land a step later, and by then the pass has restarted and collapsed the cube, so the slot reads a partial admission and recycles, every pass. The shell is one collision step of front travel thick, at a radius outside the zone; a real hand's frame-to-frame dither carries it out of the shell on its own.
- **The tilt staggers a standing column; it does not separate every pair.** Where the tags match several of a player's senders, two of them still share a slot when their tilted radii fall within one step of front travel: members close in height, a hand beside a hip for instance.
- **A new hand waits for the front, and a quick tap can be missed.** A hand is taken only when the front reaches it, up to one effective period after it enters (§Knobs, `sweepSeconds`), and one that enters and leaves within a pass can go unseen. With every slot holding a hand nothing rides: a newcomer waits until a slot frees. A hand first reached inside the zone fires the payload where it is found. A hand already held fires as it crosses into the zone, late by the frames the follower, the squares and the zone's transition take, so a fast hand fires inside the zone; widen `burstRadius` if that matters.
- **Dedup's blind spots.** Two real hands whose decoded positions sit within `dedupBand` of each other on every axis release the higher slot, which for heads is physically impossible and for hands rare. A rider that re-takes a hand whose holder is not reacquiring is matched only while both positions are current, which they are in every tracking state. Two held hands tied on an axis leave a phantom of them standing until dither or a departure breaks the tie, since the phantom rung needs the merge strictly above every other source. A merge with an unheld member is the first trap above.
- **A remote copy's receivers reach its animator about a frame later than the wearer's own, so a dwell one collision step long on the wearer is one step short on every other client.** With `LatchGrow` one step long, a copy's rider can leave it before the grown cluster's readings land, decode the placement cluster's readings with the hold cluster's coefficients, miss `dedupBand`, and take the zone rung: once per pass it fires the payload over a hand another slot already holds, for the frames until dedup releases one of the two. The wearer's own client never shows it, and neither does the emulator, whose receiver latency is exactly one frame. `latchSeconds` ships at two steps for this, and every step-spanning dwell a consumer overrides must keep the same margin.
- **Every hand already inside fires the payload again at each load, mirror clone, manual hide and show, and distance-hide resume.** The front re-acquires them from scratch and cannot tell a resident from an arrival, so an edge reader (the shipped burst, a ping) fires once per resident at each of those events while a level reader (a marker, a mosaic) simply comes back on. A consumer whose edge payload must not repeat needs its own memory of who was already there. On a late joiner's client the rig may not sense players who were already in the instance at all (`runtime.md` §Contacts).
- **A hand within a sender radius of the cage centre is never taken.** A collapsed cube still has a centre, and a hand whose surface overlaps it keeps that overlap through every collapse and restart, so no fresh episode ever begins for it. The shipped cage sits at the hips, so that volume is inside the wearer's own body.
- **The zone's ends are the cube's corners, and there is no band there.** Inside the band the full radius holds all around; above and below it the cube's reach depends on direction, so a hand high above you can be in the zone on one side and out of it on another. A hand that leaves through an end is held until it passes the bound, then drops the payload with no re-arm band, and on its way back is taken by the next pass to reach it, firing once more where it is found. Consumers whose senders sit far from the cage centre in height raise the cage (`HomeAnchor/Offset`) or grow `acqHalf`. Under three boxes a reading saturates once the hand's surface passes a cluster face, so a hand at the cluster's edge reads nearer the centre than it is until the follower catches up; `fourBox` measures the radius out and has no such bias.
- **The boundary can hide a hand that still holds the payload**: the drawn tube is the burst radius over the band, the re-arm band lies outside it, and the zone continues past the tube's ends.
- **The readout coefficients and the cluster sizes are one unit.** `followHalf`, `placeHalf` and `senderRadius` are baked into the clips; resize the boxes only through CONFIG, never on the prefab.

Deliberately not shipped: a height bound on the cylinder (caps with their own re-arm band: two more rungs per tracking state, for exits people do not make on foot; `yw` is exported, so a consumer that needs one adds it); a sphere zone; an `allowSelf` self-test variant (it changes what the pattern senses); one `Emit` shared across slots (a possible material-count saving, untested); a standing acquisition face beside the front to catch the taps it misses, since the rig is built to track hands that stay; a quiet mode for residents re-found at a load or resume (`generate.py`'s docstring has the trade); preemption, since evicting a tracked hand for a newcomer would cost a victim ladder per slot and still miss the hand it evicted; and a cluster stretched to dodge the crowd-blocking trap, which costs contact pairs of its own.

## What is not proven here

The shipping client has never run the follower hold. Everything below is open in the client:

- `pairDelay`: the evaluation on which a raw reading first reflects a move of the cluster's centre, at 60 fps, at a jittered 30 fps and at 144 fps;
- the follower's lag and error on a moving hand, and the speed at which a hand leaves the hold cluster at a crowd client's frame rate;
- the fresh latch: the admission surviving the shrink and move, and under `fourBox` the fourth box admitting through its placement and `R` reading live after the grow;
- the reacquire landing on a cut hand inside the zone with the payload level unbroken, and a rider that re-took the hand released with no second burst;
- the placement dropping a same-step merge of two hands apart by more than its reach, and what a closer merge leaves;
- the bound and the far release on a settled hand walking away;
- whether a real crowd blocks a cluster at a held hand (§Traps);
- the burst landing on the toucher's hand as the toucher and a third observer each see it, the real capsule hand's `senderRadius`, and readout dither at range, which sizes the re-arm band;
- an avatar carrying two copies (this entry beside an owned one), which stacks both receiver counts in one spot;
- sticky rejection itself, which every pass leans on (`runtime.md` §Contacts);
- whether a re-admission of a held hand fires the payload, which the client cannot show on the wire;
- a hand standing where a reacquire opens being admitted on some of its receivers and not others on the frame it opens: the reopened cluster has no partial rung, so that reacquire waits out its timeout and releases.

## Changing it

- **Regenerate the build as a unit.** Run `py -3.13 generate.py`, then recompile `built/` as a unit over the committed `.meta`s (`CONVENTIONS.md` §The gate has the procedure). The same run regenerates `assets/UnitCylinder.obj`; `--mesh` writes only the mesh.
- **Run the check every time you touch the prefab.** `py -3.13 generate.py --check` asserts the hand-maintained surface no compile or gate reads: receiver fields and tags, the tilt and its inverse, the node scales and positions the readout and dedup rest on, the seam, the World pins, the boundary. Nothing runs it for you and "gates green" never includes it.
- **Changing K.** Set CONFIG's `K`. Duplicate or delete `Slot<k>` subtrees directly under `Cage/Size`, with receiver parameters `CR/Slot<k>/…`, the tilt on the slot and its inverse on `Output`. The geometry must be exact: every slot's frame must be the same, since dedup compares positions decoded in each slot's own frame, and a slot offset or scaled by more than `dedupBand` still tracks and still bursts but stops recognising the second admissions the front makes every pass. That is why `--check` asserts every slot's position, scale and box placement. Regenerate the build; `--check` counts slots against K.
- **Turning on `fourBox`.** Add an `X−` beside each slot's `X+ Y+ Z+`: duplicate the `X+` node, turn it 180° about Y so its +Z face is the cage's −X face, and point its parameter at `CR/Slot<k>/X-`. The controller animates its scale, so leave it at one. Then regenerate, since the readout coefficients change. `--check` fails the receiver count and parameter set until every slot has it.
- **Another sender.** Set CONFIG's `tags` and every receiver's collision tags; `--check` compares them. Set `senderRadius` to the new senders' typical radius.
- **An owned copy** carries the tilt, the slot geometry and the box fields by hand, under the same exactness rule as a new slot, and regenerates its document through `document(overrides)`, which applies its overrides over this CONFIG, so a default changed here reaches the copy at its next regeneration. A key CONFIG does not carry is refused at that door, so a knob this generator drops fails a copy loudly. `check_prefab` in `generate.py` is the door a copy calls to hold the rig; it refuses a `Hit` receiver under a slot's `Boxes` by name, and the fix is to delete that node, which nothing animates.

## Verifying the install

Your own hands are excluded by design, so nothing fires when you test alone. Drive a scripted `HandR` sender (`emulator.md` §Fake another player's contact) or use two clients. Run `--check` first (§Changing it); it catches the geometry, receiver and seam faults of the prefab asset, and nothing else below.

Enable on, walk. `Cage` rides the Hips, world-aligned, at world scale 1 whatever the avatar's scale; finding it at the module's mount point means the BoneProxy never resolved. A `Marker` sits upright while its `Boxes` sit tilted; a tilted marker or upright boxes means the tilt and its inverse have come apart, the failure mode of a hand-copied rig. A menu toggle that does nothing means the enable came out of the build prefixed: `globalParams` must be exactly `[ContactRadar/Enable]`. Two instances on one avatar show as one toggle driving both. With nothing inside, one slot's cube at a time grows from the centre to the face, shrinks to nothing and grows again, indefinitely; a cube resting at full size means no controller drives the slots or it is not this generator's current output.

Put the sender inside the zone. As the front reaches it, the riding slot's `Boxes` jump to a small cluster on the sender, grow a little, and the sender bursts; the next slot's cube appears at the size the front had and grows on. A cluster that lands beside the sender rather than on it means `senderRadius` is far off the sender's radius, or a receiver sits off its `Boxes` node (`--check` names it). Drag the sender at walking speed: the cluster and `Marker` must follow it, the payload staying on. With two senders inside, at each handoff the incoming slot's shut cube must be the size the outgoing cube had on its last admitting frame; larger means the front freeze or `CR/SweepPrev` is broken. A burst that fires with nothing visible means the `Burst` to `Emit` sub-emitter link was dropped by a re-edit; it is a non-child reference Unity's inspector will not author, and nothing checks it.

Dedup shows on every pass: with one sender held inside the zone, each time the front reaches it a second slot admits it, places a cluster on it, and releases itself once its follower runs, with no second burst, while the first slot holds it throughout. A second slot that keeps it means the slots' frames differ (§Changing it). The distance-hide path is the one behaviour only a recipe exercises: set the local runtime's `IsAnimatorEnabled` false, disable its Animator a few frames later, move the senders, then re-enable both. Every `Boxes` reads collapsed from the frame after the flag, and the resume re-acquires the senders now present, each one inside the zone firing the payload.
